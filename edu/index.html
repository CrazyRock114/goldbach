🧑‍🏫 Teacher Guide (Modular Arithmetic & Symmetry): Why do multiples of 6 (like 12, 18, 24, 30, 36) have roughly twice as many Goldbach decompositions? Every prime $>3$ is of the form $6k \pm 1$. When $N \equiv 0 \pmod 6$, a prime $6a - 1$ connects with $6b + 1$ freely. When $N \equiv 2$ or $4 \pmod 6$, half of all primes are algebraically locked out from pairing!
