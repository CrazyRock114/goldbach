✨ Intuitive Concept: The Solved Cousin

While the binary (even) Goldbach conjecture remains unsolved, its odd cousin—the Ternary Goldbach Conjecture—was triumphantly PROVEN TRUE in 2013 by mathematician Harald Helfgott!

It states that every odd number larger than 5 can be written as the sum of THREE prime numbers: $p_1 + p_2 + p_3 = \text{Odd}$.

Why three primes? Because the sum of three odd primes is always odd: $\text{Odd} + \text{Odd} + \text{Odd} = \text{Odd}$! Having three prime degrees of freedom provides vastly more combinations than just two.

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 What is the smallest odd number covered by the conjecture, and what is its partition?
The smallest odd number $>5$ is 7! Its unique ternary partition is $7 = 2 + 2 + 3$ (or using three odd primes, the smallest is $9 = 3 + 3 + 3$).
Challenge 2 Set $N = 35$. How many different 3-prime partitions can you cycle through?
$N = 35$ has dozens of 3-prime combinations! For example: $3 + 3 + 29 = 35$, $3 + 13 + 19 = 35$, $5 + 7 + 23 = 35$, $7 + 11 + 17 = 35$, $11 + 11 + 13 = 35$.
Challenge 3 If the Strong (even) Goldbach is true, why must the Weak (odd) Goldbach also be true?
If $N$ is odd, then $N - 3$ is even! By the strong conjecture, every even number $N - 3 = p_1 + p_2$. Adding 3 back gives $N = 3 + p_1 + p_2$, proving the ternary conjecture instantly!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

Helfgott's Theorem (2013): Every odd integer $N \ge 7$ is the sum of three primes:

\forall N \in 2\mathbb{N} + 1, N \ge 7: \quad \exists p_1, p_2, p_3 \in \mathbb{P} \text{ such that } p_1 + p_2 + p_3 = N

The proof unified the Hardy-Littlewood-Vinogradov circle method with explicit major arc bounds for $N > 10^{27}$ and rigorous interval arithmetic / FFT verification for $N \le 10^{27}$.
