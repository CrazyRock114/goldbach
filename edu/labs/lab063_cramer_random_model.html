✨ Why Goldbach "Must" Be True Probabilistically

In 1936, Swedish mathematician Harald Cramér proposed treating primes not as rigid deterministic objects, but as a sequence of independent random trials where each integer $x$ has probability $P(x \in \mathbb{P}) = \frac{1}{\ln x}$.

For a given even integer $N$, there are approximately $N/2$ candidate pairs $(k, N-k)$. The probability that both are prime is $\approx \frac{1}{\ln^2 N}$.

Multiplying candidate pairs by this probability yields the expected number of Goldbach pairs: $\mathbb{E}[g(N)] \approx \frac{N}{2 \ln^2 N}$. Because $\frac{N}{\ln^2 N}$ grows without bound, the probability that an even number has ZERO pairs plunges exponentially like $e^{-\frac{N}{2\ln^2 N}}$!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 At $N = 1000$, what is the probability of a Goldbach failure under the Poisson model?
$\mathbb{E}[g(1000)] \approx 10.5$. The Poisson probability of zero pairs is $e^{-10.5} \approx 0.000027$ (less than 1 in 36,000)!
Challenge 2 Why does Cramér's raw model predict ~5.2 pairs for $N=120$, while actual $g(120) = 12$?
Because Cramér's model assumes independent random numbers and ignores modular divisibility! Since 120 is divisible by 3 and 5, the Hardy-Littlewood singular series multiplies the expectation by $2.67\times$, bringing the prediction to $\approx 11.8$, in near-perfect agreement with 12!
Challenge 3 Click "Roll Synthetic Universe" 5 times. Did Goldbach fail in any simulated universe?
Even with random dice rolls, the sheer number of available pair candidates almost always yields at least one (and usually many) synthetic Goldbach pairs!

📐 The Borel-Cantelli Lemma & Asymptotic Certainty

Under the Cramér random model, let $E_N$ be the event that even integer $N$ has no Goldbach representations. Assuming Poisson distribution with mean $\mu_N = \mathbb{E}[g(N)] \sim \frac{N}{2\ln^2 N}$:

P(E_N) = \exp(-\mu_N) \le \exp\left(-\frac{C N}{\ln^2 N}\right)

Because the sum $\sum_{N=4}^\infty P(E_N) < \infty$ converges rapidly, the **First Borel-Cantelli Lemma** guarantees that with probability 1, only finitely many even integers could ever fail Goldbach's conjecture.
