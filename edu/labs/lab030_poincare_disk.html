✨ Intuitive Concept: Non-Euclidean Bridges

In 1882, Henri Poincaré revolutionized mathematics by creating a universe where space is curved (hyperbolic). In this world, the shortest path between two points on the boundary is not a straight line, but a circular arc that meets the boundary at strict 90° right angles!

When you map numbers around the circle, Goldbach pairs $(p, q)$ leap inward across the disk along these curved hyperbolic bridges.

Because hyperbolic space expands exponentially toward the interior, these prime bridges pass through a rich conformal corridor, visualizing prime connections with infinite non-Euclidean elegance.

Euclidean chords are flat straight lines. Hyperbolic geodesics bend outward toward the boundary, meeting the rim at exact perpendicular ($90^\circ$) angles as required in Poincaré's conformal metric!

Primes that are closer together (like $13$ and $17$) stay shallow near the rim. Primes that are on opposite sides dive right through the deep center of hyperbolic infinity!
Challenge 3 Why does every pair $(p, N-p)$ have its closest approach to the center at the angle corresponding to $N/2$?
Because $N/2$ is the midpoint! The geodesic reaches its apex (closest distance to the origin) precisely along the radial ray pointing at $N/2$.

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

In the Poincaré disk model $\mathbb{D} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}$ equipped with the metric:

ds^2 = \frac{4 |dz|^2}{(1 - |z|^2)^2}

the boundary is $\partial\mathbb{D} = S^1$. For boundary points $\xi_1 = e^{i\theta_1}$ and $\xi_2 = e^{i\theta_2}$ with $\theta_1 = 2\pi p / N$ and $\theta_2 = 2\pi(N-p) / N$, the unique hyperbolic geodesic is the circular arc orthogonal to $\partial\mathbb{D}$.
