✨ Bubbles of Number Theory

In 1938, American mathematician Lester R. Ford defined a geometric representation of fractions: for any reduced fraction $h/k$, draw a circle tangent to the horizontal axis at $x = h/k$ with diameter $1/k^2$.

Ford discovered a breathtaking geometric truth: no two Ford circles ever overlap! They are either completely disjoint, or they touch each other with perfect tangency if and only if $|h_1 k_2 - h_2 k_1| = 1$ (adjacent terms in a Farey sequence).

In Goldbach's conjecture, decomposing $N = p + q$ splits the unit interval $[0, 1]$ into two symmetric halves: $p/N + q/N = 1$. The twin circles for $p/N$ and $q/N$ have identical radii and sit in reflection symmetry on either side of the majestic central circle at $1/2$!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Why does the circle at $x = 1/2$ have such a massive radius compared to $p/N$?
Radius is $r = 1 / (2 k^2)$. For $1/2$, the denominator is $k = 2$, so radius is $1/8 = 0.125$. For $p/24$, the denominator is 24, so radius is $1 / (2 \times 576) \approx 0.00086$ (much smaller!).
Challenge 2 Verify that twin Goldbach circles $(p/N, q/N)$ always have the exact same size.
Both fractions share the exact same reduced denominator (or identical factor of $N$). Since radius depends strictly on denominator $k$, their circles are mirror-image twins!
Challenge 3 How do Ford circles connect to the Hardy-Littlewood circle method?
In 1937, Hans Rademacher used the upper boundaries of Ford circles as the exact path of integration to solve partition asymptotic formulas, unifying circle method major arcs with hyperbolic geometry!

📐 Ford Tangency Criterion & Farey Neighbors

Let $C(h_1/k_1)$ and $C(h_2/k_2)$ be two Ford circles. The squared distance between their centers is:

d^2 = \left(\frac{h_1}{k_1} - \frac{h_2}{k_2}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{1}{2k_1^2} - \frac{1}{2k_2^2}\right)^2 = (r_1 + r_2)^2 + \frac{(h_1 k_2 - h_2 k_1)^2 - 1}{k_1^2 k_2^2}

Hence $d = r_1 + r_2 \iff |h_1 k_2 - h_2 k_1| = 1$. The boundary of all mutually tangent Ford circles tiles the hyperbolic upper half-plane under the modular group $\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$.
