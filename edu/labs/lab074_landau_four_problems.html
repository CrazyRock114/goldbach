Lab 074: Landau's Four Unsolved Prime Problems

At the 1912 International Congress of Mathematicians in Cambridge, Edmund Landau highlighted four foundational prime conjectures that he declared "unattackable in the present state of science". Over a century later, all four remain open!

1. Goldbach's Conjecture UNSOLVED

Can every even integer $N > 2$ be written as the sum of two primes?

38 = 7 + 31 (2 pairs)
2. Twin Prime Conjecture UNSOLVED

Are there infinitely many primes $p$ such that $p + 2$ is also prime?

#5: (29, 31)
3. Legendre's Conjecture UNSOLVED

Does there always exist a prime between $n^2$ and $(n+1)^2$?

Between 36 and 49: Primes {37, 41, 43, 47}
4. Landau's n² + 1 Problem UNSOLVED

Are there infinitely many primes of the polynomial form $n^2 + 1$?

10² + 1 = 101 (PRIME!)

✨ The Four Pillars of Prime Mystery

In 1912, German mathematician Edmund Landau stood before the Fifth International Congress of Mathematicians in Cambridge and listed these four questions, stating that contemporary mathematics lacked the tools to solve any of them.

More than 110 years later, despite the development of modern sieve theory, the circle method, and arithmetic geometry, all four problems remain officially open!

Notice what they share: each asks about the existence or infinity of primes under simple polynomial operations (addition $p+q=2k$, shifts $p+2$, squares $n^2 < p < (n+1)^2$, and quadratic polynomials $n^2 + 1$).

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Test Legendre's conjecture for $n = 1$ up to $n = 10$. Did every single square interval contain at least one prime?
Yes! In fact, the number of primes in $[n^2, (n+1)^2]$ grows as $n$ grows (for $n=6$, there are 4 primes: 37, 41, 43, 47). Yet no one can prove it never drops to zero!
Challenge 2 In Problem 4, find 5 values of $n$ where $n^2 + 1$ is prime.
$n=1$ ($1^2+1=2$), $n=2$ ($2^2+1=5$), $n=4$ ($4^2+1=17$), $n=6$ ($6^2+1=37$), $n=10$ ($10^2+1=101$), $n=14$ ($14^2+1=197$), $n=16$ ($16^2+1=257$).
Challenge 3 Which of the four problems was closest to being solved?
Twin Primes and Goldbach: Yitang Zhang proved bounded gaps ($p_{n+1} - p_n \le 7 \times 10^7$) in 2013, and Chen Jingrun proved $N = p + P_2$ for Goldbach!

📐 The Sieve Obstruction Across All Four Problems

All four of Landau's problems suffer from the same deep technical barrier:

\text{Parity Obstruction: } \mu(n) = (-1)^{\Omega(n)} \text{ cannot be detected by linear sieve weights } \lambda_d

Because classical sieve theory can only bound numbers having at most $r$ prime factors (almost primes), crossing the threshold from $r=2$ to $r=1$ requires non-sieve arithmetic input (such as bilinear forms or modular curve geometry).
