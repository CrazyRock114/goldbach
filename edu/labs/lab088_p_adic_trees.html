Lab 088: $p$-Adic Numbers & Hensel's Lemma

What if two numbers are considered "close" when their difference is divisible by a high power of prime $p$? Step into Kurt Hensel's $p$-Adic Ultrametric Universe and explore tree branching modulo $p, p^2, p^3$!

Topology: ULTRAMETRIC CANAL

p-Adic Metric & Valuation

Valuation Definition:
|x|ₚ = p^{ -vₚ(x) }

Divisible by $p^3 \implies \text{Distance} = 1/p^3$ (closer to 0 than $1/p$!).

Strong Triangle Inequality:
|x + y|ₚ ≤ max(|x|ₚ, |y|ₚ)

Every triangle in the $p$-adic plane is isosceles!

💡 Hensel's Lemma: If a polynomial congruence $f(x) \equiv 0 \pmod p$ has a simple root, it can be lifted uniquely to a solution in $\mathbb{Z}_p$, just like Newton's method!

✨ An Upside-Down Distance

In standard real analysis, large numbers like 1,000,000 are huge and far away from 0. But in the 3-adic world, the number $3^5 = 243$ is tiny: its distance to 0 is only $|243|_3 = \frac{1}{243} \approx 0.004$!

Invented in 1897 by Kurt Hensel, $p$-adic numbers treat divisibility by $p$ as physical closeness. Two numbers are close if they agree on their first few base-$p$ digits.

In modern number theory, solving equations globally over $\mathbb{Q}$ begins by solving them locally in every $p$-adic field $\mathbb{Q}_p$ (the Hasse Local-Global Principle). The singular series $\mathfrak{S}(N)$ in Goldbach's conjecture is literally the product of all local $p$-adic densities!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Which number is closer to 0 in the 5-adic metric: 25 or 100?
$25 = 5^2 \implies |25|_5 = 1/25 = 0.04$. $100 = 4 \times 5^2 \implies |100|_5 = 1/25 = 0.04$. They are at the exact same distance to 0! But $125 = 5^3 \implies |125|_5 = 1/125 = 0.008$ is much closer!
Challenge 2 Why is every triangle in a $p$-adic space strictly isosceles?
Because of the Ultrametric Inequality $|x + y|_p \le \max(|x|_p, |y|_p)$. If $|x|_p \ne |y|_p$, equality MUST hold: $|x + y|_p = \max(|x|_p, |y|_p)$, meaning the two largest sides are always equal!
Challenge 3 How does the $p$-adic perspective explain the multiple-of-6 bonus?
In $\mathbb{Q}_3$, when $3 \mid N$, the local measure of solutions to $p + q = N$ doubles compared to when $3 \nmid N$. Multiplying local densities over all primes yields the Hardy-Littlewood formula!

📐 Local Solvability & The Hardy-Littlewood Singular Series

Let $\mu_p(N) = \lim_{k \to \infty} \frac{p^k \#\{x \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})^\times \mid N - x \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^k\mathbb{Z})^\times\}}{\phi(p^k)^2}$ be the local $p$-adic Haar measure. Direct calculation yields:

\mu_p(N) = \begin{cases} 1 - \frac{1}{(p-1)^2} & \text{if } p \nmid N \\ 1 + \frac{1}{p-1} = \frac{p-1}{p-2} \left(1 - \frac{1}{(p-1)^2}\right) & \text{if } p \mid N \end{cases}

The Euler product over all finite primes $\prod_{p} \mu_p(N)$ converges precisely to the singular series $\mathfrak{S}(N)$, proving that the Goldbach comet envelope is the product of local $p$-adic volumes.
