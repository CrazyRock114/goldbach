✨ A 15-Year-Old Prodigy's Masterpiece

In 1792, young Carl Friedrich Gauss received a copy of Johann Heinrich Lambert's book of mathematical tables, which included a table of logarithms and a list of primes up to 100,000.

By laboriously counting primes in blocks of 1,000, Gauss made a startling discovery: primes do not appear erratically without law; their average density near $x$ decreases exactly like the reciprocal of the natural logarithm, $\frac{1}{\ln x}$!

Summing up these probabilities gives the Logarithmic Integral $\text{Li}(x) = \int_2^x \frac{dt}{\ln t}$. Because the probability that $p$ is prime is $1/\ln p$ and $q = N-p$ is prime is $1/\ln(N-p)$, the expected number of Goldbach pairs is $\int \frac{dt}{\ln t \ln(N-t)} \approx \frac{N}{\ln^2 N}$!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 At $x = 1000$, how many true primes exist, and what does Gauss's $\text{Li}(x)$ predict?
$\pi(1000) = \mathbf{168}$. Gauss's $\text{Li}(1000) \approx \mathbf{177.6}$. An error of less than 6%, astonishing for an asymptotic estimate!
Challenge 2 Why does $\text{Li}(x)$ consistently overestimate $\pi(x)$ for small numbers?
For small $x$, $\text{Li}(x) > \pi(x)$. Remarkably, J. E. Littlewood proved in 1914 that the difference $\pi(x) - \text{Li}(x)$ changes sign infinitely many times, though the first crossover occurs at the astronomical Skewes number ($>10^{316}$)!
Challenge 3 How does the $1/\ln^2 N$ term in Goldbach originate directly from Gauss's discovery?
Since Goldbach requires TWO independent primes $p$ and $N-p$, the joint probability is $\frac{1}{\ln N} \times \frac{1}{\ln N} = \frac{1}{\ln^2 N}$.

📐 The Prime Number Theorem & Error Term

The Prime Number Theorem (Hadamard & de la Vallée Poussin, 1896) asserts:

\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\pi(x)}{\text{Li}(x)} = 1, \quad \text{Li}(x) = \int_2^x \frac{dt}{\ln t} = \frac{x}{\ln x} + \frac{x}{\ln^2 x} + \mathcal{O}\left(\frac{x}{\ln^3 x}\right)

Under the Riemann Hypothesis, the error is bounded by $|\pi(x) - \text{Li}(x)| \le \frac{1}{8\pi} \sqrt{x} \ln x$, providing the precision required to bound major arcs in Goldbach circle method estimates.
