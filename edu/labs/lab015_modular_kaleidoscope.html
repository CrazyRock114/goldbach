✨ Intuitive Concept: Geometric Mandalas

A kaleidoscope works by reflecting light across mirrors positioned at regular angles. In mathematics, modular arithmetic acts as a numeric kaleidoscope!

When you wrap the number line around a circle of size $m$, every prime lands on one of the residue spokes. For a chosen even number $N$, connecting prime pairs $p + q = N$ draws chords across the mandala.

When $m$ is a multiple of 6 (like 12, 30, 60), the chords exhibit striking bilateral and rotational symmetries, proving visually that Goldbach pairs are not random chaos, but structured geometric reflections!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Set $m = 12$ and $N = 60$. Notice the vertical mirror symmetry of all chords.
Since $60 \equiv 0 \pmod{12}$, any pair satisfies $p + q \equiv 0 \pmod{12}$, meaning $q \equiv -p \pmod{12}$. In circular coordinates, $-p$ is the exact mirror reflection across the vertical axis!
Challenge 2 Switch to $m = 30$ and set $N = 30$. What is the geometric relationship between the 3 Goldbach chords?
The chords $(7, 23)$, $(11, 19)$, and $(13, 17)$ are all perfectly parallel horizontal rungs! Each chord connects symmetric pairs across the 15-axis.
Challenge 3 What happens when $N$ is NOT divisible by $m$ (e.g. $N = 64$ with $m = 12$)?
The symmetry axis tilts! Since $64 \equiv 4 \pmod{12}$, the reflection axis rotates by $4/2 = 2$ units, mirroring across the 2–8 axis instead of the 0–6 vertical axis.

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

The circle group $\mathbb{T} = \mathbb{R} / \mathbb{Z}$ has finite cyclic subgroups $C_m \cong \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$. The reflection map corresponding to Goldbach decomposition $p + q \equiv N \pmod m$ is the affine involution:

\sigma_N: \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}, \quad \sigma_N(x) = N - x

The fixed points satisfy $2x \equiv N \pmod m$. Chords $(x, \sigma_N(x))$ form perpendicular trajectories to the axis of symmetry $\theta_0 = \frac{\pi N}{m}$.
