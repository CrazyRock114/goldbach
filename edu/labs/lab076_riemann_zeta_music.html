✨ Prime Numbers as Standing Waves

In classical acoustics, a musical note is composed of a fundamental tone plus an infinite ladder of overtones (harmonics).

In 1859, Bernhard Riemann discovered that prime numbers are the nodes of a cosmic standing wave! The frequencies of these waves are given by the imaginary parts of the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function:

\rho_k = \frac{1}{2} + i \gamma_k, \quad \gamma \in \{14.1347, 21.0220, 25.0109, 30.4249, 32.9351, \dots\}

Each zero contributes a wave term $-2 x^{1/2} \frac{\cos(\gamma_k \ln x)}{\gamma_k}$. As you add more harmonics with the slider, watch the waves cancel each other out in composite regions and spike upwards at the primes $2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13$!

📐 Riemann's Explicit Formula & Goldbach Major Arcs

Riemann's exact formula for Chebyshev's prime counting function $\psi(x) = \sum_{p^k \le x} \ln p$ expresses the discrete prime count in terms of the spectrum of zeros:

\psi(x) = x - \sum_{\rho} \frac{x^\rho}{\rho} - \ln(2\pi) - \frac{1}{2}\ln(1 - x^{-2})

In Hardy-Littlewood-Vinogradov circle method proofs of Goldbach conjectures, the exponential sum $S(\alpha) = \sum_{p \le N} e^{2\pi i p \alpha}$ is decomposed using these exact spectral zeros, proving that major arc resonances dominate the integral.
