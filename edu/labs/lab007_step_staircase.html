✨ Intuitive Concept: The Skyline of Numbers

Imagine walking up a grand staircase in a mathematical city. Each step is an even number: 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, and so on.

Built upon each step is a tower of blocks: each block is a unique prime pair that sums to that number! As you look across the skyline, you'll see a fascinating pattern:

  • The Solo Four (4, 6, 8, 12) are humble one-story cottages (1 pair).
  • Multiples of 6 (18, 24, 30, 36, 42...) shoot up like glistening skyscrapers!
  • Powers of 2 (16, 32, 64...) dip down into low valleys because they possess no odd prime factors.

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Climb up to $N = 30$. How many stories high is its tower?
$N = 30$ has 3 pairs: $7+23, 11+19, 13+17$. (It is divisible by both 2, 3, and 5—the first primorial $2 \times 3 \times 5 = 30$—giving it maximum prime abundance!)
Challenge 2 Compare the tower of $N = 30$ with its neighbors $N = 28$ and $N = 32$. What do you notice?
$N = 28$ has 2 pairs ($5+23, 11+17$). $N = 32$ has only 2 pairs ($3+29, 13+19$). 30 towers above both of its immediate neighbors!
Challenge 3 Look across the entire staircase up to 80. Are there any steps where the stack height is ZERO?
NEVER! Every single step has a stack height of at least 1. If a step ever had height zero, that would be a counter-example disproving Goldbach's Conjecture!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

The Goldbach counting function $g(N)$ forms a discrete sequence over even integers $2\mathbb{N}_{\ge 4}$:

g(N) = \sum_{p \le N/2} \mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(p) \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(N - p)

The sequence $g(N)$ is non-monotonic: it oscillates wildly due to the prime factors of $N$. By the Hardy-Littlewood asymptotic formula, $g(N)$ scales directly with the singular series factor:

\mathfrak{S}(N) = 2 C_2 \prod_{p \mid N, p > 2} \frac{p-1}{p-2}

which achieves strict local maxima when $\gcd(N, 6) = 6$ and local minima when $N = 2^k$.
