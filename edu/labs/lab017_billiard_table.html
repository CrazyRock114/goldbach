✨ Intuitive Concept: The Hexagonal Table

Mathematical billiards studies the trajectory of a friction-free particle bouncing off polygonal walls.

On this 6-sided table, the walls represent residues modulo 6. Only two pockets are Prime Pockets (Pocket 1 and Pocket 5). The other four pockets (0, 2, 3, 4) are blocked by bumpers.

When you aim a cue shot for even target $N$, the ball must launch from one Prime Pocket $p$ and sink cleanly into another Prime Pocket $q$ such that $p + q = N$!

The 3 shots are: $(5, 19)$, $(7, 17)$, and $(11, 13)$. Notice that in every shot, one prime is $6k-1$ and the other is $6k+1$!
Challenge 2 Set $N = 22$ ($N \equiv 4 \pmod 6$). Which pockets do the primes launch from?
$22 = 5 + 17$ (both $6k-1 \equiv 5 \pmod 6$) or $3 + 19$ (pocket 3 is 3). Since $5 + 5 \equiv 10 \equiv 4 \pmod 6$, both primes must land in Pocket 5!
Challenge 3 Why does Pocket 3 only open when one of the primes is literally the number 3?
Because 3 is the only prime divisible by 3! Any other number landing on Pocket 3 is a multiple of 3 ($6k+3 = 3(2k+1)$) and therefore composite!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

In a dynamical billiard system $(\Omega, \mu, T)$ where $\Omega$ is a regular hexagon, the collision sequence $\{s_k\}$ on sides $\{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ models symbolic dynamics.

s_0 + s_1 \equiv N \pmod 6 \quad \text{with } s_0, s_1 \in \{1, 5\}

The billiard flow preserves the invariant measure and illustrates that valid Goldbach pairs correspond to non-ergodic periodic orbits connecting unit residue walls.
