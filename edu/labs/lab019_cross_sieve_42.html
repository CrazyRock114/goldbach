Lab 019: The 2·3·7 = 42 Cross-Sieve

Famous in pop culture as the "Answer to the Ultimate Question," $42 = 2 \times 3 \times 7$ holds a profound secret in number theory: how divisibility by 7 creates an extra 1.20× resonance layer in the Goldbach comet!

42 × 1 = 42
7-Divisibility Bonus: +20% (6/5)
The 7-Column Residue Sieve for N = 42:
Singular Series Factor
2.0 (mod 3) × 1.20 (mod 7) = 2.40×

4 pairs

✨ Intuitive Concept: The Seventh Harmonic

You already know that multiples of 6 get a $2.0\times$ boost because they escape divisibility by 3.

What happens when a number is ALSO divisible by 7? For example, 42, 84, 126, 168...

Because $7 \mid N$, candidate primes do NOT knock out any residue modulo 7 ($N - r \not\equiv 0 \pmod 7$). All 6 coprime residues survive, providing an additional multiplier of:

\frac{7 - 1}{7 - 2} = \frac{6}{5} = 1.20 \quad (+20\% \text{ bonus!})

Combined with the $2.0\times$ boost from 3, multiples of 42 enjoy a total boost of $2.40\times$!

Challenge 2 Why does $N = 42$ have MORE pairs than $N = 40$ (which is close in size)?
$40 = 2^3 \times 5$. It is NOT divisible by 3 (loses the $2\times$ boost) and NOT divisible by 7 (loses the $1.2\times$ boost). 42 is divisible by both 3 and 7, so it outperforms 40!
Challenge 3 Slide to $42 \times 2 = 84$. How many pairs does 84 have?
$N = 84$ has 8 distinct Goldbach pairs! ($5+79, 11+73, 13+71, 17+67, 23+61, 31+53, 37+47, 41+43$).

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

The Euler product for the singular series $\mathfrak{S}(N)$ contains independent factors for each odd prime $p$ dividing $N$:

\prod_{p \mid N, p > 2} \frac{p-1}{p-2} = \left(\frac{3-1}{3-2}\right) \times \left(\frac{7-1}{7-2}\right) = 2 \times \frac{6}{5} = \frac{12}{5} = 2.40

This strictly dictates the stratification of the Goldbach comet into distinct secondary hyper-dense parabolic layers.
