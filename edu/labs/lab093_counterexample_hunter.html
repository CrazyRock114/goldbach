✨ Why the Smallest Prime is So Small

If Goldbach's conjecture were false for some even number $N$, then every prime $p \le N/2$ would have $N - p$ composite.

How likely is that? If $N = 100,000$, there are 5,133 primes below $N/2$. For $g(N) = 0$, all 5,133 independent trials would have to simultaneously fail! The probability of this occurring by chance is smaller than $10^{-500}$.

In practice, as this hunter engine shows, the algorithm almost never has to look past the first few primes. For 99.9% of all numbers, $p_{\min}(N) \le 109$, demonstrating the overwhelming abundance of prime pairs!

📐 Linnik's Theorem & The Least Prime Bound

By Linnik's Theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, the least prime in a residue class $a \pmod q$ satisfies $p_{\min} \ll q^L$ (where $L \le 5$).

p_{\min}(2k) \ll (\ln 2k)^2 \quad \text{(heuristically under Cramér)}

Because $p_{\min}(2k)$ grows only poly-logarithmically, the verification search consumes virtually constant time per integer on average, enabling astronomical empirical verification.
