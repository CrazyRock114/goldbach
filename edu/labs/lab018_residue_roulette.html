✨ Intuitive Concept: The Random Prime Casino

By the Prime Number Theorem, a random integer near $x$ has a chance of roughly $1 / \ln x$ of being prime.

If whether $p$ and $N - p$ are prime were two completely independent coin flips, the probability of both being prime would be:

P(\text{both prime}) \approx \frac{1}{\ln N} \times \frac{1}{\ln N} = \frac{1}{\ln^2 N}

Since there are $N/2$ candidate pairs to test, the expected number of Goldbach pairs would be roughly:

\mathbb{E}[g(N)] \approx \frac{N}{2 \ln^2 N}

Because $N / \ln^2 N \to \infty$ as $N \to \infty$, the probability of an even number having ZERO pairs shrinks exponentially to zero!

Challenge 1 Click "Spin 100 Times" for $N = 48$. How many times do both numbers hit primes?
$48$ has 5 valid prime pairs ($5+43, 7+41, 11+37, 17+31, 19+29$) out of 11 prime candidates $\le 24$. That gives a true hit rate of $5 / 11 \approx 45.5\%$!
Challenge 2 Why does Cramér's pure random model underestimate the true number of pairs for multiples of 6?
Because prime events are NOT completely independent! As we learned in Lab 012, modular arithmetic creates strong correlations: multiples of 6 escape divisibility by 3 for both numbers simultaneously, doubling the true probability!
Challenge 3 As $N$ grows from 20 to 100, what happens to the total number of expected pairs?
Even though the probability of an individual number being prime ($1/\ln N$) slowly decreases, the number of candidate pairs ($N/2$) grows much faster (linearly). So total pairs increase steadily!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

In Cramér's probabilistic model of primes (1936), integers are modeled by independent random variables $X_n \in \{0, 1\}$ with $\mathbb{P}(X_n = 1) = \frac{1}{\ln n}$ for $n \ge 3$. The sum of two random primes has expectation:

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{3 \le p \le N/2} X_p X_{N-p}\right] \sim \int_3^{N/2} \frac{dt}{\ln t \ln(N - t)} \sim \frac{N}{2 \ln^2 N}

Hardy and Littlewood corrected this heuristic by introducing the singular series $\mathfrak{S}(N)$, exactly accounting for modular dependency across all prime moduli.
