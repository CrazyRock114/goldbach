Lab 011: The 6k ± 1 Gear Mechanism

Why do all prime numbers greater than 3 live exclusively on just TWO numbers: $6k-1$ and $6k+1$? Turn the 6-toothed mechanical gear to see four of the six teeth completely blocked by 2 and 3!

17
Residue Modulo 6: 5 (6k - 1)
Formula:
17 = 6 × 3 - 1
Status:
PRIME (Lands on Tooth 5)

✨ Intuitive Concept: The 4 Locked Doors

Imagine a revolving door with 6 chambers, corresponding to remainders when dividing by 6: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.

  • Chamber 0: Multiples of 6 ($6k$) — divisible by 2 and 3! (Locked 🔒)
  • Chamber 2: $6k + 2 = 2(3k + 1)$ — even, divisible by 2! (Locked 🔒)
  • Chamber 3: $6k + 3 = 3(2k + 1)$ — divisible by 3! (Locked 🔒)
  • Chamber 4: $6k + 4 = 2(3k + 2)$ — even, divisible by 2! (Locked 🔒)

Four out of the six chambers are permanently locked! Therefore, every single prime number larger than 3 is forced to exit through Chamber 1 ($6k+1$) or Chamber 5 ($6k-1$). This simple fact governs the entire universe of Goldbach sums!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Test $n = 25$ and $n = 35$. They land on teeth 1 and 5, but are they prime?
$25 = 5 \times 5$ ($6 \times 4 + 1$) and $35 = 5 \times 7$ ($6 \times 6 - 1$). They are composite! The $6k \pm 1$ rule is a necessary condition for primes $>3$, but not sufficient (multiples of 5, 7, 11 also land here).
Challenge 2 What are the only two primes that do NOT fit the $6k \pm 1$ rule?
The primes 2 and 3! 2 has remainder 2 mod 6, and 3 has remainder 3 mod 6. They are the divisors that lock the other four doors!
Challenge 3 If $p$ and $q$ are both $6k-1$ primes, what residue mod 6 does their sum $p+q$ have?
$(6k_1 - 1) + (6k_2 - 1) = 6(k_1 + k_2) - 2 \equiv 4 \pmod 6$. For example, $5 + 11 = 16 = 6 \times 2 + 4$!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

The multiplicative group of units modulo 6 is:

(\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z})^\times = \{[1], [5]\} \quad (\text{order } \phi(6) = 2)

Since $\gcd(p, 6) = 1$ for all $p \in \mathbb{P} \setminus \{2, 3\}$, Dirichlet's theorem on arithmetic progressions guarantees:

\pi(x; 6, 1) \sim \pi(x; 6, 5) \sim \frac{1}{2} \text{Li}(x)

Primes $>3$ are asymptotically partitioned equally between residues 1 and 5 mod 6.
