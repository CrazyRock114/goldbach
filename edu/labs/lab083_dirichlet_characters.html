✨ Complex Numbers Sifting Primes

In 1837, Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet achieved the first rigorous proof that any arithmetic progression $a, a+m, a+2m, \dots$ contains infinitely many primes, provided $\gcd(a, m) = 1$.

His secret weapon was a set of multiplicative homomorphisms $\chi: (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times \to \mathbb{C}^\times$.

Because these functions satisfy the Orthogonality Relation, summing over all characters $\chi$ causes every residue class except the target class $a$ to destructively interfere and sum to exactly ZERO! In modern Goldbach sieves, characters modulo 3 and 6 algebraically isolate the $6k \pm 1$ prime pairs.

Challenge 1 Select $m = 4$. What are the values of the non-trivial character $\chi_1(n)$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$?
$\chi_1(1) = 1$, $\chi_1(2) = 0$ (not coprime), $\chi_1(3) = -1$, $\chi_1(4) = 0$. This is the famous alternating character separating primes $4k+1$ from $4k+3$!
Challenge 2 Why does $\sum_{\chi} \chi(a) \overline{\chi(b)} = 0$ when $a \not\equiv b \pmod m$?
Because the characters form the Pontryagin dual group of $(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$. The sum over any non-trivial group representation is identically zero!
Challenge 3 Select $m = 5$. Notice the complex values $\pm i$. How do they cancel out?
The vectors point in 4 orthogonal compass directions ($1, i, -1, -i$). Their vector sum is $1 + i - 1 - i = 0$, achieving perfect balanced cancellation!

📐 Group Character Orthogonality Relations

Let $G = (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^\times$ with dual group $\widehat{G}$. The first and second orthogonality relations state:

\frac{1}{\phi(m)} \sum_{\chi \in \widehat{G}} \chi(a) \overline{\chi(b)} = \delta_{a, b \pmod m}, \quad \sum_{a \in G} \chi_1(a) \overline{\chi_2(a)} = \phi(m) \delta_{\chi_1, \chi_2}

These relations allow decomposing the prime indicator $\mathbf{1}_{p \equiv a}$ into an explicit sum of Dirichlet $L$-functions $L(s, \chi) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\chi(n)}{n^s}$.
