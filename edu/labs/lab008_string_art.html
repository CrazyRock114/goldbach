✨ Intuitive Concept: String Art Geometry

In circular string art (popular in craft and mathematics), pins are hammered into a circular wooden board, and colored thread is stretched between pairs of pins.

When you connect pins whose labels are primes summing to $N$, you create a Goldbach Chord Pattern! In cumulative mode, as you weave threads for all even numbers up to $N$, the overlapping chords form a striking curved envelope inside the circle.

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Switch to Cumulative Envelope and slide $N$ up to 100. What shape begins to emerge in the dark center?
A cardioid / nephroid caustic envelope! Because prime density gradually decreases towards the top of the circle, the caustic curves focus chord intersections into a bright inner crescent.
Challenge 2 In Single Number mode, test $N = 30$, $42$, and $60$. Notice how chord chords cross near the center.
These numbers are highly composite multiples of 6 ($30 = 6 \times 5$, $42 = 6 \times 7$, $60 = 6 \times 10$), so they possess many symmetric prime pairs, creating a dense web of crisscrossing chords!
Challenge 3 Click the "Strum String Harp" button. How does the pitch of each string correspond to its primes?
Longer chords (connecting distant primes like $3 + 45$) produce deeper, lower fundamental tones, while shorter chords (primes near $N/2$) produce higher, chime-like harmonics!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

Let the boundary circle be parameterized by angle $\theta(k) = 2\pi \frac{k}{M}$ for $k \in \{0, \dots, M-1\}$. For primes $p, q$ satisfying $p + q = N$, the chord is the line segment:

L(p, q) = \{(1 - t) e^{i\theta(p)} + t e^{i\theta(q)} \mid t \in [0, 1]\}

The family of chords $\bigcup_{N \le M} \{L(p, q) : p+q = N, p, q \in \mathbb{P}\}$ forms a discrete envelope whose limit curve corresponds to the Legendre-Goldbach caustic.
