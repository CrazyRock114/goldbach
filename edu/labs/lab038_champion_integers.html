Lab 038: The Champion Integers Hall of Fame

Meet the record-breaking "Champion Integers" that set brand-new all-time highs for the smallest Goldbach prime $p_{\min}(N)$! Step through mathematical history to see who pushed the boundary furthest.

Champion Spotlight:

N = 98 (Record p_min = 19)

Winning Partition: 19 + 79 = 98

98 is the undisputed champion of the two-digit numbers! It resists the first 7 prime keys (3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17), holding the all-time record until 220.

✨ Intuitive Concept: Record-Breaking Champions

In sports, champions break world records for high jumps or sprints. In number theory, Champion Integers break world records for resisting small prime keys!

For $N = 4$, the record is $p=2$.
For $N = 6$, the record jumps to $p=3$.
For $N = 12$, it reaches $p=5$.
For $N = 30$, it reaches $p=7$.
For $N = 98$, it makes a giant leap to $p=19$!

Notice how slowly the records climb: you have to travel all the way past 900 to find a number whose smallest prime key is 73!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Why is 10 NOT in the Hall of Fame?
Because for $N=10$, $p_{\min}(10) = 3$ ($3+7=10$). But the record of 3 was ALREADY established earlier by $N=6$! A number only enters the Hall of Fame if it strictly exceeds all previous records.
Challenge 2 Click on Champion 220. What is its record $p_{\min}$?
$p_{\min}(220) = 23$ ($23 + 197 = 220$). It beats 98's record of 19!
Challenge 3 Why are many champions divisible by 2 and other small primes?
If $N$ shares divisors with small numbers, $N - p$ will also be divisible by those numbers when $p$ shares residues, knocking out multiple small prime candidates simultaneously!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

An integer $N^* \in 2\mathbb{N}_{\ge 4}$ is a Goldbach Champion Integer if:

p_{\min}(N^*) > \max_{4 \le N < N^*} p_{\min}(N)

The sequence of champions $\{4, 6, 12, 30, 98, 220, 308, 556, 992, \dots\}$ forms OEIS sequence A025018. The record values $p_{\min}(N^*)$ form OEIS sequence A025019.
