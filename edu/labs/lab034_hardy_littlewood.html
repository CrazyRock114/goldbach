✨ Intuitive Concept: The Circle Method Miracle

Before 1923, mathematicians could only guess how many Goldbach pairs a number would have. Godfrey Harold Hardy and John Edensor Littlewood made a breathtaking breakthrough by applying complex analysis and Fourier series on the unit circle.

Their formula predicts that $g(N)$ is proportional to $N / \ln^2 N$, scaled by a modular fingerprint $\mathfrak{S}(N)$ composed of factors $\frac{p-1}{p-2}$ for each odd prime dividing $N$.

As you test numbers in this calculator, notice how astonishingly close the formula comes to reality—often within 1% to 2%!

Because the error term in the Circle Method is of lower asymptotic order ($O(N / \ln^3 N)$). As $N \to \infty$, the relative error shrinks to zero!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

The Hardy-Littlewood Conjecture A (1923) asserts that for even $N \ge 4$:

g(N) \sim 2 C_2 \left(\prod_{p \mid N, p > 2} \frac{p - 1}{p - 2}\right) \int_2^{N-2} \frac{dt}{\ln t \ln(N - t)}

where $C_2 = \prod_{p > 2} \left(1 - \frac{1}{(p-1)^2}\right) \approx 0.6601618158$ is the twin prime constant. Asymptotically, the integral is equivalent to $\frac{N}{\ln^2 N}$.
