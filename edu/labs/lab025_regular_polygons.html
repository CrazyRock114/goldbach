✨ Intuitive Concept: The Parallel Ladder

A regular polygon has equal sides and equal angles. When you label the vertices $0, 1, 2, \dots, N-1$, the midpoint $N/2$ sits exactly on the opposite side of 0.

Any two vertices $p$ and $q$ that sum to $N$ satisfy:

p + q = N \implies q = N - p

In circular geometry, the chord connecting $p$ and $N - p$ is always perpendicular to the axis joining 0 and $N/2$! This means all Goldbach chords run strictly parallel to one another, like the rungs of a ladder!

Because vertex 0 is zero, and 0 is not a prime!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

The vertices of a regular $N$-gon are roots of unity $\zeta_k = e^{2\pi i k / N}$ for $k \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}$. Chords connecting $p$ and $N-p$ have midpoint coordinates:

M(p) = \frac{\zeta_p + \zeta_{N-p}}{2} = \frac{e^{2\pi i p/N} + e^{-2\pi i p/N}}{2} = \cos\left(\frac{2\pi p}{N}\right) \in \mathbb{R}

All midpoints lie along the real axis $\text{Im}(z) = 0$, rigorously proving that every Goldbach chord is strictly orthogonal to the diameter axis.
