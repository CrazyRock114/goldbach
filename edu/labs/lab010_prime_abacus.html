✨ Intuitive Concept: Dual Wire Balancing

The abacus is one of humanity's earliest counting tools. On this Prime Bead Abacus, each brass wire is calibrated to prime positions.

You control both wires independently. As you slide the top bead $p$ to the right, the bottom bead $q$ must adjust to keep the balance beam aligned with target weight $N$. If both numbers land on prime notches, the mechanical frame locks into place!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Set Target $N=20$. Drag beads to discover both balanced prime configurations.
The two balanced solutions are: $3 + 17 = 20$ and $7 + 13 = 20$.
Challenge 2 Try Target $N=30$. How many distinct balanced settings can you find?
There are 3 solutions: $7 + 23 = 30$, $11 + 19 = 30$, and $13 + 17 = 30$.
Challenge 3 Why are there no prime notches for even numbers $>2$ on either wire?
Because every even number greater than 2 is divisible by 2 and therefore composite!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

The prime abacus defines a discrete constrained optimization on the discrete product manifold $\mathbb{P} \times \mathbb{P} \subset \mathbb{N}^2$:

\Phi(p, q) = |p + q - N|

Equilibrium is defined by the zero-energy locus $\Phi(p, q) = 0$. Goldbach's conjecture asserts that the zero locus is non-empty for all $N \in 2\mathbb{N}_{\ge 4}$.
