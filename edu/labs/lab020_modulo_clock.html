✨ Intuitive Concept: Clock Face Arithmetic

You use modular arithmetic every single day when you look at a wall clock! If it is 9 o'clock and you wait 5 hours, $9 + 5 = 14 \equiv 2$ o'clock.

On this Modulo 12 Clock, two hands point to prime numbers $p$ and $q$. Their angle on the dial represents their remainder when divided by 12.

When the combined rotation of both hands equals $N \pmod{12}$, the dial locks into an algebraic resonance: a valid prime partition!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 On a 12-hour clock, which positions can primes $>3$ land on?
Only 4 positions: 1, 5, 7, and 11! All other 8 positions are divisible by 2 (0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10) or by 3 (3, 9). Notice: $\phi(12) = 4$!
Challenge 2 Set $N = 24$. Notice that $24 \equiv 0 \pmod{12}$. How must the two clock hands balance?
They must be mirror images across the 12 o'clock vertical axis ($p + q \equiv 0 \equiv 12 \pmod{12}$)! For example, 5 o'clock + 7 o'clock = 12 o'clock, or 1 o'clock + 11 o'clock = 12 o'clock!
Challenge 3 Click "Align Hands to Solution" to test $N = 36$. How many pairs can the clock display?
$36$ has 4 pairs: $5+31$, $7+29$, $13+23$, and $17+19$. Each creates a balanced bilateral reflection on the clock face!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

Let $C_{12} \cong \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$. The set of coprime residues is $(\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z})^\times = \{1, 5, 7, 11\}$. Every prime $p > 3$ satisfies:

p \in \{1, 5, 7, 11\} \pmod{12}

Goldbach partitions satisfy the cyclic group congruence $p + q \equiv N \pmod{12}$. The Klein 4-group structure $(\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z})^\times \cong C_2 \times C_2$ guarantees involutive symmetry for all $N \equiv 0 \pmod{12}$.
