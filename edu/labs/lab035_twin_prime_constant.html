✨ Intuitive Concept: An Infinite Prime Symphony

Imagine multiplying infinite fractions together, one for each odd prime:

C_2 = \left(1 - \frac{1}{2^2}\right) \times \left(1 - \frac{1}{4^2}\right) \times \left(1 - \frac{1}{6^2}\right) \times \dots

For $p = 3$: $\frac{3}{4} = 0.75$.
For $p = 5$: $0.75 \times \frac{15}{16} \approx 0.7031$.
For $p = 7$: $0.7031 \times \frac{35}{36} \approx 0.6836$.

Because each fraction is just slightly less than 1, the product gently settles down to an exact cosmic limit: $0.6601618...$. Double this number ($2 C_2 \approx 1.3203$), and you get the exact coefficient for the bottom floor of the Goldbach Comet!

Challenge 2 Why is $p=2$ excluded from the product?
Because if $p=2$, the denominator $(p-1)^2 = (2-1)^2 = 1$, and $1 - \frac{1}{1} = 0$, which would zero out the entire product! The prime 2 is factored out into the leading coefficient 2.
Challenge 3 Where else in number theory does $C_2$ appear?
In the Twin Prime Conjecture! The estimated number of twin primes up to $x$ is $2 C_2 \int_2^x \frac{dt}{\ln^2 t}$. The exact same constant governs both twin primes and Goldbach pairs!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

The Twin Prime Constant $C_2$ is defined by the Euler product:

C_2 = \prod_{p > 2} \left(1 - \frac{1}{(p - 1)^2}\right) = \prod_{p > 2} \frac{p(p - 2)}{(p - 1)^2} \approx 0.6601618158468695739278...

Its relationship to the Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s)$ can be computed via accelerated series: $\ln C_2 = \sum_{k=2}^\infty \frac{-2^k + 2}{k} (\sum_{p>2} p^{-k})$.
