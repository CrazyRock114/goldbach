Lab 081: Elliptic Curves over Finite Fields $E(\mathbb{F}_p)$

Elliptic curves power modern cryptography and solved Fermat's Last Theorem! Decompose even number $N$ into a Goldbach pair $(p, q)$, and compare the elliptic curves $y^2 \equiv x^3 + ax + b$ defined over the finite fields $\mathbb{F}_p$ and $\mathbb{F}_q$.

✨ From Continuous Curves to Modular Constellations

Over real numbers $\mathbb{R}$, an elliptic curve $y^2 = x^3 + ax + b$ is a smooth looping line. But over a finite field $\mathbb{F}_p$, it becomes a scattered constellation of discrete grid points!

Because $p$ and $q$ are the twin prime components of a Goldbach sum $p + q = N$, we can define the identical algebraic curve over both fields.

Notice how each field exhibits vertical reflection symmetry across the line $y = p/2$ (since $(-y)^2 \equiv y^2 \pmod p$). Points on these modular curves form an abelian group, providing the foundational algebra for modern ECC encryption and the Wiles-Taylor proof of Fermat's Last Theorem!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Verify that every point $(x, y)$ in the left panel satisfies $y^2 \equiv x^3 + 2x + 3 \pmod 7$.
Take $x = 2$: $x^3 + 2x + 3 = 8 + 4 + 3 = 15 \equiv 1 \pmod 7$. The quadratic residues of 1 mod 7 are $y = 1$ and $y = 6$ ($1^2 \equiv 1, 6^2 \equiv 36 \equiv 1$). Thus $(2, 1)$ and $(2, 6)$ are valid points!
Challenge 2 Why does the number of points on $E(\mathbb{F}_p)$ always respect Hasse's Theorem?
Helmut Hasse proved in 1934 that the number of points satisfies $|\#E(\mathbb{F}_p) - (p + 1)| \le 2\sqrt{p}$. This is the finite-field analogue of the Riemann Hypothesis!
Challenge 3 Switch $N$ to 30 (primes 11 and 19). Does the larger prime always produce more curve points?
Generally yes! Since $\#E(\mathbb{F}_p) \approx p + 1$, the larger prime $q = 19$ has roughly twice as many points as $p = 11$.

📐 Hasse's Theorem & Modularity

For any elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{F}_p$ with discriminant $\Delta = -16(4a^3 + 27b^2) \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$:

\#E(\mathbb{F}_p) = p + 1 - a_p, \quad \text{where } |a_p| \le 2\sqrt{p}

The trace of Frobenius $a_p$ generates the local factor $L_p(E, s) = (1 - a_p p^{-s} + p^{1-2s})^{-1}$ of the global $L$-function. The Modularity Theorem (Wiles et al.) connects these prime traces directly to Fourier coefficients of weight-2 modular forms.
