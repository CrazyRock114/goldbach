✨ Intersecting Diophantine Clues

In a standard crossword, interlocking letters constrain word possibilities. In a Prime Cross-Number Puzzle, intersecting digits simultaneously satisfy horizontal and vertical Goldbach equations!

For example, if 1-Across is a two-digit prime $p$ such that $p + 19 = 50$, the answer is 31. The tens digit 3 immediately supplies the first digit for 1-Down, while the units digit 1 constrains 2-Down!

This illustrates how number-theoretic relationships cross-validate each other across shared decimal representations.

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Calculate 1-Across: "The larger prime partner of 17 in the partition of 54".
$54 - 17 = \mathbf{37}$. Since 37 is prime, the answer is 37!
Challenge 2 Why can no two-digit prime ending in 5 appear in any Goldbach clue?
Any two-digit number ending in 5 is divisible by 5 (e.g. 15, 25, 35...) and therefore composite. The only prime ending in 5 is the single digit 5 itself!
Challenge 3 Solve the entire 5×5 cross-number grid without using the Reveal button.
Solve 3-Across ($N=20$) and 4-Across ($N=30$) first; their prime complements provide fixed anchors for the vertical columns.

📐 Base-10 Digit Constraints & Primes

A multi-digit integer $D = \sum_{k=0}^{m-1} d_k 10^k$ is subject to both global primality criteria and local positional digit constraints:

d_0 \in \{1, 3, 7, 9\} \quad (\forall D \in \mathbb{P}, D > 5), \quad \sum_{k=0}^{m-1} d_k \not\equiv 0 \pmod 3

Combining positional congruences with the Goldbach condition $D_1 + D_2 = 2k$ generates tight, finite solution trees that can be solved via branch-and-bound constraint programming.
