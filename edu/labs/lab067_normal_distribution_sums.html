✨ Why Addition Breeds the Bell Curve

The Central Limit Theorem states that when independent random variables are added together, their normalized sum converges toward a Gaussian normal distribution (bell curve).

Although prime numbers are completely deterministic, their distribution along the number line mimics pseudo-random noise. When we compute the sum $S = p_1 + p_2$ for all primes $p_1, p_2 \le M$, the probability density of the sum is the convolution of two distributions.

This creates a smooth, symmetric bell curve centered at the mean $\mu \approx M$, with maximum pair density right in the middle! The tooth-like ripples along the curve reflect the multiple-of-6 modular resonances.

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Why does the peak of the histogram occur near $N \approx M$?
Because the average prime in the pool is approximately $M / 2$. Adding two such primes yields an expected sum of $M/2 + M/2 = M$!
Challenge 2 Increase $M$ from 50 to 200. How does the bell curve change?
The curve widens ($\sigma$ grows) and smooths out, as the larger sample of primes makes the Central Limit approximation even more pronounced.
Challenge 3 Why are there gaps (zero bars) between adjacent bars in the histogram?
Because almost all odd integers cannot be formed by adding two odd primes! The histogram is dominated by even sums, leaving odd sums near zero.

📐 Convolution of Prime Densities & Lyapunov CLT

Let $X_1, X_2$ be random variables drawn from the prime counting measure $d\pi(x) \sim \frac{dx}{\ln x}$. The density function of their sum $Z = X_1 + X_2$ is the convolution:

f_Z(z) = (f_{X_1} * f_{X_2})(z) = \int_2^{z-2} \frac{dt}{\ln t \cdot \ln(z - t)}

By the Lyapunov central limit condition, the asymptotic envelope of $f_Z(z)$ converges to a Gaussian kernel $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ where $\mu = 2 \mathbb{E}[P] \approx M$ and $\sigma^2 = 2 \operatorname{Var}(P) \approx \frac{M^2}{6}$.
