Core Conjecture
Goldbach's Conjecture
Christian Goldbach & Leonhard Euler (1742)
2N = p + q \quad (\forall N \ge 2)
Every even integer greater than 2 is the sum of two prime numbers. Formulated in Goldbach's letter to Euler on June 7, 1742.
Direct Goldbach Connection: The central unsolved jewel of additive number theory that drove the invention of the Circle Method, Large Sieve, and modular methods.

Classroom Story: The 300-Year Constellation

Mathematics is not a collection of isolated islands—it is an interconnected web of breakthroughs.

When Euler received Goldbach's letter in 1742, modern analysis barely existed. To tackle Goldbach, mathematicians had to invent Generating Functions, the Riemann Zeta Function, the Hardy-Littlewood Circle Method, and Selberg's Sieve Theory.

Even though the binary conjecture remains unproven, its gravity pulled forward almost every major field of modern number theory!

Atlas Discovery Challenge

Challenge 1: Trace the path from Hardy & Littlewood (1923) to Harald Helfgott (2013). Which intermediate breakthrough proved that every large odd number is the sum of 3 primes?

Ivan Vinogradov (1937) invented novel trigonometric sum estimates to prove unconditionally that all sufficiently large odd numbers are sums of 3 primes. Helfgott completed the journey in 2013 by reducing the threshold and verifying the remainder with computers.

Challenge 2: What crucial bridge connects Chen's Theorem ($p + P_2$) with Yitang Zhang's bounded prime gaps?

Both rely fundamentally on the Bombieri-Vinogradov Theorem and advanced sieve methods (Selberg sieve & linear sieve) that overcome the parity barrier in distribution of primes in arithmetic progressions.

Taxonomy of Additive Number Theory

Number theory approaches to Goldbach divide into three primary strategic pillars:

$$\text{Analytic: } \int_0^1 \left(\sum p e^{2\pi i p \alpha}\right)^2 e^{-2\pi i (2N) \alpha} d\alpha > 0$$ $$\text{Sieve Theory: } S(A, \mathcal{P}, z) = \sum_{a \in A, (a, P(z))=1} 1$$ $$\text{Density / Bases: } \sigma(A) > 0 \implies kA = \mathbb{N}$$

Whenever one pillar encountered an obstruction (e.g., the parity barrier or generalized Riemann hypothesis), researchers switched pillars to advance the frontier.
