✨ Intuitive Concept: The Key of 3

In mathematics, when you want to solve a new problem, you try to reduce it to a problem you already understand.

Suppose the Strong Goldbach Conjecture is true (every even number is the sum of two primes). If anyone hands you ANY odd number $M \ge 7$:

  1. You simply subtract 3: $M - 3$.
  2. Since $\text{Odd} - \text{Odd} = \text{Even}$, the remainder $M - 3$ is guaranteed to be an even number $\ge 4$!
  3. By the Strong Conjecture, this even number splits into two primes: $M - 3 = p_1 + p_2$.
  4. Add 3 back to both sides: $M = 3 + p_1 + p_2$!

You have just proven the Weak Goldbach Conjecture for that odd number in four simple steps!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Test $M = 31$. What is the even remainder, and what two primes unlock it?
$31 - 3 = 28$ (an even number!). The even number 28 splits into $11 + 17$ (or $5 + 23$). Thus, $31 = 3 + 11 + 17$!
Challenge 2 Why can't we subtract 2 instead of 3?
Because $\text{Odd} - 2 = \text{Odd}$! The remainder would still be odd, so we couldn't use the Strong (even) Goldbach conjecture on it. We must subtract an ODD prime (like 3) to convert the odd target into an even remainder!
Challenge 3 Could we subtract 5 or 7 instead of 3?
Yes! For any odd prime $p$, $M - p$ is even. But subtracting 3 works for the widest possible range of odd numbers ($M \ge 7$).

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

Theorem (Strong $\implies$ Weak): Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the assertion $\forall n \in 2\mathbb{N}_{\ge 4}, \exists p_1, p_2 \in \mathbb{P}: p_1 + p_2 = n$. Then:

\mathcal{S} \implies \left(\forall m \in 2\mathbb{N} + 1, m \ge 7: \exists p_1, p_2, p_3 \in \mathbb{P}: p_1 + p_2 + p_3 = m\right)

Proof: Given odd $m \ge 7$, let $n = m - 3$. Since $m \equiv 1 \pmod 2$, $n \equiv 0 \pmod 2$ and $n \ge 4$. By $\mathcal{S}$, $n = p_1 + p_2$. Thus $m = 3 + p_1 + p_2$, where $3, p_1, p_2 \in \mathbb{P}$. $\blacksquare$
