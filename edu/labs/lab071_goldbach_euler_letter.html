Euler's Historic Reply (30 June 1742):
"Dass ein jeder numerus par eine summa duorum primorum seye, halte ich vor ein ganz gewisses theorema, ungeachtet ich dasselbe nicht demonstriren kann."
("That every even integer is a sum of two primes, I regard as a completely certain theorem, although I cannot prove it.")

✨ Why 1 Was Considered Prime in 1742

Modern students are taught that 1 is neither prime nor composite. But in Christian Goldbach's era, 1 was universally classified as a prime number!

Because 1 was considered prime, Goldbach's original statement was phrased for all integers: "Every integer greater than 2 can be written as the sum of three primes."

For example, in Goldbach's convention, $4 = 1 + 1 + 2$ and $6 = 1 + 2 + 3$. When Euler reformulated it using modern conventions ($p \ge 2$), he noted that it is equivalent to stating that every even number is the sum of two primes! This became the standard formulation known across the globe today.

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Click on the "three primes" passage in the letter. What was Goldbach's example for 4?
Goldbach wrote $4 = 1 + 1 + 2$. Using 1 as a prime, 4 is the sum of three primes!
Challenge 2 Why was 1 eventually removed from the definition of prime numbers in the 19th and 20th centuries?
To preserve the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic (unique prime factorization)! If 1 were prime, $6 = 2 \times 3 = 1 \times 2 \times 3 = 1 \times 1 \times 2 \times 3$, destroying unique factorization.
Challenge 3 Read Euler's reply. Did Euler believe the conjecture was true?
Yes! Euler called it "ein ganz gewisses theorema" (a completely certain theorem), yet lamented that he could find no formal mathematical proof.

📐 Equivalence of Goldbach's and Euler's Formulations

Under the historical convention $\mathbb{P}_{\text{old}} = \mathbb{P} \cup \{1\}$, Goldbach conjectured:

\forall n \in \mathbb{N}, n > 2: \quad \exists p_1, p_2, p_3 \in \mathbb{P}_{\text{old}} \text{ s.t. } p_1 + p_2 + p_3 = n

Euler observed that setting $p_3 = 1$ reduces every even number $2k$ to $p_1 + p_2 = 2k - 1 + 1 = 2k$. Modern formulation restricts $\mathbb{P} = \{2, 3, 5, \dots\}$, stating $\forall 2k \ge 4, \exists p_1, p_2 \in \mathbb{P}: p_1 + p_2 = 2k$.
