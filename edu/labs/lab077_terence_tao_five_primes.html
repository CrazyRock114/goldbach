Lab 077: Terence Tao's 2012 Theorem: Odd Numbers as $\le 5$ Primes

In 2012, Fields Medalist Terence Tao proved that every odd integer greater than 1 is the sum of at most FIVE prime numbers. Enter any odd number to decompose it into its minimal prime basis!

Theorem: Tao (2012) Proven
Phase 1: Vinogradov Major Arcs
Using the circle method with smoothed prime weights, Tao established that exponential sums around rational points $a/q$ yield positive main terms for all $N > 10^{30}$.
Phase 2: Additive Sieve & Type II Sums
By combining Selberg's sieve with bilinear form estimates, Tao proved that parity obstructions cannot prevent a 5-prime representation.
Phase 3: Finite Verification
Collaborating with computational number theorists, the lower interval $N \le 10^{30}$ was exhaustively verified, completing the theorem unconditionally!

✨ The Stepping Stone to Weak Goldbach

Before Terence Tao's 2012 paper, it was known that every sufficiently large odd number was the sum of 3 primes (Vinogradov 1937), but the required threshold $10^{1346}$ was too vast to verify by computer.

Instead of aiming directly for 3 primes, Tao asked a pragmatic question: Can we unconditionally prove a bound of 5 primes for ALL odd numbers, down to 3?

By accepting 5 primes instead of 3, Tao gained two extra degrees of freedom! This softened the analytical bounds enough to bridge the gap between asymptotic theory and computer verification, providing the exact blueprint Harald Helfgott used to conquer 3 primes in 2013.

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Enter $N = 77$. How many primes are actually required?
Even though Tao guaranteed $\le 5$ primes, $77 = 3 + 7 + 67$ can easily be made with just 3 primes!
Challenge 2 Can ANY odd number require more than 5 primes under Tao's theorem?
No! Tao's theorem is unconditional: every odd integer $\ge 3$ can be expressed as the sum of at most 5 primes.
Challenge 3 Why are odd numbers of the form $N = \text{Prime}$ trivial for Tao's theorem?
If $N$ is prime (like 17 or 31), it is already the sum of 1 prime! $1 \le 5$, satisfying the theorem automatically.

📐 Formal Statement of Tao's Theorem (2012)

Let $\mathbb{P}$ be the set of primes.

\forall N \in 2\mathbb{N} + 1, N \ge 3: \quad \exists k \in \{1, 3, 5\} \text{ and } p_1, \dots, p_k \in \mathbb{P} \text{ such that } \sum_{i=1}^k p_i = N

The proof introduced a modified von Mangoldt function $\Lambda_R(n)$ and established that the Fourier transform $\widehat{\Lambda_R}(\alpha)$ concentrates entirely on major arcs of bounded denominator.
