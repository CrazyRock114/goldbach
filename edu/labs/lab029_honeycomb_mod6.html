✨ Intuitive Concept: The Hexagonal Hive

In nature, bees build hexagonal hives because hexagons tile the plane with the least perimeter. In number theory, hexagons are special because every hexagon has exactly 6 neighbors!

This 6-fold geometry mirrors arithmetic modulo 6. As numbers spiral through the hive, prime numbers concentrate exclusively in the $6k-1$ and $6k+1$ radial corridors.

Connecting prime pairs that sum to $N$ reveals symmetric honey paths that link opposite honeycomb corridors!

They all sit along two specific radial angles ($60^\circ$ and $300^\circ$) corresponding to remainders 1 and 5 mod 6!
Challenge 2 Set $N = 24$. Follow the path connecting 11 and 13. How close are they in the hive?
They are twin primes ($11$ and $13$), separated by only 2! In the hive, they sit in neighboring rings directly across the midpoint 12.
Challenge 3 Why are multiples of 6 (like 6, 12, 18, 24) always located in the exact same straight line in the hive?
Because each full spiral cycle of 6 hexes advances by 6! All multiples of 6 align on the positive horizontal ray.

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

The triangular lattice $A_2$ dual to the hexagonal tiling can be parameterized by Eisenstein integers $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ with $\omega = e^{2\pi i / 3}$. Primes $p \equiv \pm 1 \pmod 6$ map to units:

(\mathbb{Z}[\omega] / 6\mathbb{Z}[\omega])^\times

The Goldbach connection vector $v = z_p + z_{N-p}$ exhibits rotational equivariance under the dihedral symmetry group $D_6$.
