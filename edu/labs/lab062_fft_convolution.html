✨ The Convolution Theorem & Modern Verification

In 1998, Tomás Oliveira e Silva began one of the greatest distributed computing campaigns in history: systematically verifying that every even integer up to $4 \times 10^{18}$ satisfies Goldbach's conjecture.

Testing numbers one by one via trial division would have taken longer than the age of the universe! Instead, mathematical engines break the number line into intervals and compute prime sums using Discrete Fourier Transform convolutions.

Because convolution in the time domain corresponds to simple element-wise multiplication in the frequency domain, the Fast Fourier Transform (Cooley-Tukey algorithm) slashes computational complexity from quadratic $O(N^2)$ to nearly linear $O(N \log N)$!

📐 The Discrete Convolution Theorem

Let $\mathbf{a} \in \{0, 1\}^N$ be the characteristic vector where $a_k = 1 \iff k \in \mathbb{P}$. The cyclic convolution $(\mathbf{a} * \mathbf{a})_n = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} a_k a_{n-k}$ satisfies:

\mathbf{a} * \mathbf{a} = \mathcal{F}^{-1}\left( \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{a}) \odot \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{a}) \right) = \mathcal{F}^{-1}\left( |\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{a})|^2 \right)

Because the Discrete Fourier Transform $\mathcal{F}$ is computed in $\Theta(N \log N)$ complex operations, all representations $r_2(2k)$ across the entire interval $[4, N]$ are generated simultaneously in a single inverse transform pass.
