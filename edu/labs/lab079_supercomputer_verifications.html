✨ The Incredible Journey to 4 Quintillion

How did Tomás Oliveira e Silva and his team verify two billion billion even numbers?

They didn't check every prime pair. Instead, they used a fast **least prime hunter** strategy: for each even number $2k$, find the smallest prime $p$ such that $2k - p$ is prime.

Because the smallest prime $p_{\min}(2k)$ is almost always tiny (less than 1,000 for virtually all numbers under $4 \times 10^{18}$), verifying each even integer takes on average fewer than 3 prime tests! Using bit-parallel table lookups, their cluster processed billions of numbers per second without finding a single failure.

📐 Algorithmic Complexity of Fast Sieve Verification

To verify the interval $[N, N + \Delta]$, the algorithm maintains an offset bitset $B$ of size $\Delta$. For each small prime $p \le p_{\max}$:

B[k] \leftarrow B[k] \lor \mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(N + 2k - p)

The time complexity per even integer is $\mathcal{O}\left( \mathbb{E}[p_{\min}] \right) = \mathcal{O}(\ln^2 N)$, enabling amortized linear throughput across distributed hardware architectures.
