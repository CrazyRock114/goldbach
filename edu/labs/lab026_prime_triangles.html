Build right-angled triangles whose legs are prime numbers $p$ and $q$ summing to $N$. Which prime pair encloses the largest possible area, and which stretches into an ultra-sharp needle?

✨ Intuitive Concept: The AM-GM Area Peak

If you hold a piece of string of fixed length $N$, how should you bend it into two perpendicular legs $p$ and $q$ to catch the most area?

By the Arithmetic-Geometric Mean (AM-GM) inequality:

\sqrt{pq} \le \frac{p + q}{2} = \frac{N}{2} \implies \text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}pq \le \frac{N^2}{8}

The closer $p$ and $q$ are to the midpoint $N/2$, the squarer and more capacious the triangle becomes! The further apart they are (like $3$ and $N-3$), the skinnier the triangle.

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Test $N = 28$. Its pairs are $(5, 23)$ and $(11, 17)$. Which triangle has the larger area?
• Triangle $(5, 23)$: $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(5)(23) = 57.5$.
• Triangle $(11, 17)$: $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(11)(17) = 93.5$!
The $(11, 17)$ triangle is closer to $N/2 = 14$, so it achieves much larger area!
Challenge 2 Set $N = 10$. What kind of triangle does its $(5, 5)$ pair produce?
An Isosceles Right Triangle ($45^\circ-45^\circ-90^\circ$)! Since $p = q = 5$, it attains the exact theoretical maximum area: $10^2 / 8 = 12.5$.
Challenge 3 Can the hypotenuse $c = \sqrt{p^2 + q^2}$ ever be an integer?
Only if $(p, q, c)$ forms a Pythagorean triple! But in any primitive Pythagorean triple, one leg must be a multiple of 4 and one leg a multiple of 3. The only prime that is a multiple of 4 is none, so a right triangle of two odd primes can never have an integer hypotenuse!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

Let $\mathcal{T}_N = \{(p, q) \in \mathbb{P}^2 \mid p \le q, p + q = N\}$. The area functional is:

\mathcal{A}(p) = \frac{1}{2} p (N - p)

Since $\frac{d^2\mathcal{A}}{dp^2} = -1 < 0$, $\mathcal{A}(p)$ is strictly concave on $[0, N]$ with global maximum at $p = N/2$. The discrete set of Goldbach areas forms an ascending sequence ordered by proximity $|p - N/2| \to 0$.
