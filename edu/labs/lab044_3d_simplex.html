✨ Intuitive Concept: The 3D Triangular Slice

In 2D, the equation $x + y = N$ is a 1D diagonal line. But in 3D, adding a third prime dimension $x + y + z = N$ forms a 2D triangular plane slice (a simplex)!

The three corners of this equilateral triangle touch the axes at $(N, 0, 0)$, $(0, N, 0)$, and $(0, 0, N)$.

Every point inside this triangle whose three coordinates are all prime numbers represents a valid Weak Goldbach partition! Because a 2D surface is vastly larger than a 1D line, odd numbers have a huge abundance of 3-prime solutions.

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Rotate the 3D view using the slider. Where does the center triple sit?
Right at the barycentric center of the triangle! If $N/3$ is prime (like $N=9 \implies 3+3+3$ or $N=21 \implies 7+7+7$), a prime star sits directly at the center of the simplex!
Challenge 2 Set $N = 9$. How many prime triples lie on the simplex?
Using odd primes, there is $3 + 3 + 3 = 9$. Using 2, there are permutations of $2 + 2 + 5 = 9$.
Challenge 3 Why does the number of solutions on the simplex grow quadratically ($O(N^2)$)?
Because you can choose $p_1$ and $p_2$ independently from roughly $\pi(N)$ primes each, giving $(\pi(N))^2 \sim N^2 / \ln^2 N$ candidate pairs, so the total surface density scales with area!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

The standard 2-simplex scaled by $N$ is:

\Delta_N^2 = \{(x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^3 \mid x + y + z = N\}

The set of ternary Goldbach representations is $\mathcal{G}_3(N) = \Delta_N^2 \cap \mathbb{P}^3$. The Hardy-Littlewood asymptotic formula confirms that $\#\mathcal{G}_3(N) \sim \frac{1}{2} \mathfrak{S}_3(N) \frac{N^2}{\ln^3 N}$.
