✨ Intuitive Concept: The Packing Problem

Imagine carpenter dominoes measured in prime inches: 2", 3", 5", 7", 11", 13", and so on.

Goldbach's Conjecture says that no matter how long the even strip is, you can ALWAYS find exactly TWO prime dominoes that lay end-to-end to cover it seamlessly!

Some lengths only have one winning combination, but richer lengths (like 24 or 30) have three or four different domino pairings!

Challenge 1 On Level 1 (Target 10), find both valid domino pairings.
Pair 1: $3 + 7 = 10$. Pair 2: $5 + 5 = 10$ (two 5-dominoes!).
Challenge 2 On Level 4 (Target 24), find all 3 prime domino pairs.
The pairs are: $5 + 19 = 24$, $7 + 17 = 24$, and $11 + 13 = 24$.
Challenge 3 Why can you never use a 2-domino when the target strip is $\ge 6$?
Because if one domino is 2, the second domino would need to be an even number $N - 2$. But the only even prime domino in existence is 2, and $2 + 2 = 4 < 6$!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

Let the target strip be the closed interval $I = [0, N]$ with $N \in 2\mathbb{N}_{\ge 4}$. A prime domino tiling is an ordered pair $(p, q) \in \mathbb{P}^2$ such that:

[0, p] \cup [p, p+q] = [0, N] \quad \text{with } [0, p] \cap (p, p+q] = \emptyset

This is mathematically equivalent to the linear Diophantine relation $p + q = N$ over $\mathbb{P} \times \mathbb{P}$. The game demonstrates the guarantee of solutions for every level $N$.
