Lab 014: The Forbidden Residues Inspector

Why can't primes land on just any remainder? Inspect modular lockouts across moduli $m = 3, 5, 7, 11$ and see which residue channels are strictly forbidden from producing Goldbach pairs!

28
Open Channels: 1 / 3

✨ Intuitive Concept: The Lockout Sieve

When searching for primes $p + q = N$, we must avoid creating a complement $q = N - p$ that is divisible by a small prime like 3, 5, or 7.

Consider modulus 3. If $N = 28$ ($28 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$):

  • If $p \equiv 1 \pmod 3$, then $q = 28 - p \equiv 1 - 1 \equiv 0 \pmod 3$. Divisible by 3! FORBIDDEN 🚫!
  • If $p \equiv 0 \pmod 3$, $p$ is itself a multiple of 3. (Only $p=3$ works).
  • If $p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$, then $q \equiv 1 - 2 \equiv -1 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$. ALLOWED ✔!

This leaves only ONE open channel for large primes: $p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Set $N = 30$ and Modulus = 3. How many open channels are there?
Since $30 \equiv 0 \pmod 3$, if $p \equiv 1$, then $q \equiv 0 - 1 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$ (not 0). If $p \equiv 2$, then $q \equiv 1 \pmod 3$ (not 0). Both non-zero channels ($p \equiv 1$ and $p \equiv 2$) are OPEN! That is 2 open channels instead of 1, yielding the $2\times$ bonus!
Challenge 2 Switch to Modulus 5. What happens when $5 \mid N$ (e.g. $N=30$ or $N=50$)?
When $5 \mid N$, all 4 units $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ remain open! When $5 \nmid N$, one unit residue is knocked out ($N - r \equiv 0 \pmod 5$), leaving only 3 open channels. The ratio of open channels is $4 / 3 \approx 1.33\times$!
Challenge 3 Why does the number of open channels always equal $p-1$ when $p \mid N$, and $p-2$ when $p \nmid N$?
Modulo $p$, there are $p-1$ non-zero residues. When $p \nmid N$, one of these non-zero residues $r$ has complement $N - r \equiv 0 \pmod p$, knocking out a second residue! Thus only $(p-1) - 1 = p-2$ survive. When $p \mid N$, the complement $N - r \equiv 0 - r \equiv -r \not\equiv 0$, so zero units are knocked out by 0!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

For an odd prime $p$, consider the number of solutions to $x + y \equiv N \pmod p$ with $x, y \in (\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^\times$:

c_p(N) = \begin{cases} p - 1 & \text{if } p \mid N \\ p - 2 & \text{if } p \nmid N \end{cases}

The local density factor at prime $p$ is $\frac{c_p(N)}{p - 1}$, which yields $1$ if $p \mid N$ (relative to max) and $\frac{p-2}{p-1}$ if $p \nmid N$. Inverting gives the Euler factor $\frac{p-1}{p-2}$ in the singular series.
