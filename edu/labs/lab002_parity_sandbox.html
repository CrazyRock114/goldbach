Lab 002: The Parity Arithmetic Sandbox (Odd + Odd = Even)

Why does Goldbach's Conjecture ask only about EVEN numbers? Explore visual 2-row tiles to understand why primes (except 2) must always fuse into perfectly paired even rectangles!

Odd (3) + Odd (5) = Even (8)

✨ Intuitive Concept: The "Lonely Tile" Rule

Think of numbers arranged in two neat parallel rows (pairs of dancing partners):

  • An even number has every tile paired up into $2 \times k$ columns. No tile is left alone.
  • An odd prime always has one "lonely tile" sticking out at the end ($2k + 1$).

When you add two odd primes, their two lonely tiles find each other and pair up! The result has zero leftovers, creating a complete, unbroken even rectangle of height 2.

What if you use the prime 2? 2 is already completely even (1 column of 2 tiles, zero lonely tiles). Adding 2 to an odd prime leaves that odd prime's lonely tile alone, so $2 + \text{odd} = \text{odd}$!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Select $p=2$ and $q=2$. Is the sum even or odd? How does the tile display look?
$2 + 2 = 4$. Both numbers are even, having 0 lonely tiles. They stack together into a $2 \times 2$ square. This is the only Goldbach pair that uses the even prime 2!
Challenge 2 Select $p=2$ and $q=5$. What happens to the lonely tile? Can an even number $>4$ ever use 2?
$2 + 5 = 7$, which is ODD! Because $N$ is even, if $N = 2 + q$, then $q = N - 2$ would have to be an even prime (since Even - Even = Even). But 2 is the ONLY even prime! Therefore, the only even number with $2$ as a summand is $2 + 2 = 4$. For all $N \ge 6$, both primes MUST be odd!
Challenge 3 If $p$ and $q$ are odd primes, why is their midpoint $m = (p+q)/2$ always an integer?
Because $p + q$ is always even! Any even number is divisible by 2, which guarantees that $(p+q)/2$ is a whole number.

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

In modular arithmetic, the parity of an integer is its equivalence class modulo 2:

\mathbb{Z} / 2\mathbb{Z} = \{[0], [1]\}

Since the only even prime is 2, the set of odd primes is $\mathbb{P}_{\text{odd}} = \mathbb{P} \setminus \{2\}$. For any $p, q \in \mathbb{P}_{\text{odd}}$:

p \equiv 1 \pmod 2, \quad q \equiv 1 \pmod 2 \\ p + q \equiv 1 + 1 \equiv 2 \equiv 0 \pmod 2

Thus, $p+q \in 2\mathbb{N}$. Hence, every pair of odd primes necessarily sums to an even integer $\ge 6$.
