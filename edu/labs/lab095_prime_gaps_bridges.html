✨ Why Gaps Don't Break Goldbach

A prime gap $g_n = p_{n+1} - p_n$ is a continuous stretch of consecutive composite integers. Can an exceptionally wide prime gap ever "swallow" all Goldbach pairs and create a counter-example?

The answer is no! To create a prime gap of size $G$, you need $G - 1$ consecutive numbers to be composite.

But to satisfy Goldbach for an even number $N$, you only need one single prime $p$ in $[2, N/2]$ whose partner $N - p$ happens to be prime! Because the set of primes is scattered, the least prime $p_{\min}(N)$ almost always stays under 20, easily hopping across even the widest composite chasms.

Challenge 1 Find the largest prime gap below 500. Where does it occur?
Between 113 and 127! There is a gap of 14 consecutive composite numbers.
Challenge 2 At $N = 98$, why is $p_{\min} = 19$?
Every prime below 19 fails: $98 - 3 = 95$ ($5 \times 19$), $98 - 5 = 93$ ($3 \times 31$), $98 - 7 = 91$ ($7 \times 13$), $98 - 11 = 87$ ($3 \times 29$), $98 - 13 = 85$ ($5 \times 17$), $98 - 17 = 81$ ($3^4$). Finally $98 - 19 = 79$ (prime!).

📐 Cramér's Gap Conjecture vs. Baker-Harman-Pintz

The maximal prime gap satisfies unconditionally $p_{n+1} - p_n \ll p_n^{0.525}$ (Baker, Harman, Pintz 2001). Under the Riemann Hypothesis, $p_{n+1} - p_n \ll \sqrt{p_n} \ln p_n$.

p_{\min}(2k) \ll (\ln 2k)^2 \ll \max_{p_n \le 2k} (p_{n+1} - p_n)

Because the least Goldbach prime grows like $\mathcal{O}(\ln^2 N)$, it is asymptotically negligible compared to the square root prime gaps, ensuring robust additive connectivity.
