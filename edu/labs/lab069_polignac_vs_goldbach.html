✨ Twin Conjectures: Addition vs. Subtraction

In 1849, French mathematician Alphonse de Polignac made a bold claim: for any even number $2k$, there are infinitely many pairs of consecutive or non-consecutive primes with difference $p - q = 2k$.

  • $2k = 2$: The Twin Prime Conjecture ($3, 5$), ($5, 7$), ($11, 13$), ($17, 19$)...
  • $2k = 4$: The Cousin Prime Conjecture ($3, 7$), ($7, 11$), ($13, 17$), ($19, 23$)...
  • $2k = 6$: The Sexy Prime Conjecture ($5, 11$), ($7, 13$), ($11, 17$), ($13, 19$)...

While Goldbach searches inside a closed box $[0, 2k]$ with a finite number of primes, Polignac searches the infinite open road $[0, \infty)$. Yet remarkably, both share the exact same singular series multiplier $\mathfrak{S}(2k)$!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Set $2k = 6$. Why does Polignac's list have so many sexy prime pairs so early?
Because 6 is a multiple of 3! Primes $>3$ reside in residue classes $6k \pm 1$. The difference between $6k+1$ and $6(k-1)+1$ is 6, making difference 6 the single most common gap between consecutive primes!
Challenge 2 Can Goldbach ever have infinitely many pairs for a fixed $2k$?
Never! Since primes are positive, $p \ge 2$ and $q \ge 2$, both primes must satisfy $2 \le p, q \le 2k - 2$. There are only finitely many integers in this interval!
Challenge 3 What historic breakthrough did Yitang Zhang prove in 2013 regarding Polignac's conjecture?
Zhang proved that there is SOME even number $2k < 70,000,000$ that has infinitely many prime pairs! (Later reduced by Maynard and Tao to $2k \le 246$).

📐 Identical Singular Series in Hardy-Littlewood

The Hardy-Littlewood Conjectures give asymptotic estimates for both sums and differences:

\pi_{2k}(x) \sim 2 C_2 \prod_{p \mid 2k, p > 2} \frac{p-1}{p-2} \int_2^x \frac{dt}{\ln^2 t}, \quad g(2k) \sim C_2 \prod_{p \mid 2k, p > 2} \frac{p-1}{p-2} \frac{2k}{\ln^2(2k)}

The arithmetic factor $\mathfrak{S}(2k) = \prod_{p \mid 2k, p > 2} \frac{p-1}{p-2}$ is identical in both formulas! Multiples of 6 receive the exact same statistical advantage in twin-like gaps as they do in Goldbach partitions.
