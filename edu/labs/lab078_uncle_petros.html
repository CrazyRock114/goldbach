✨ Literature Meets Mathematical Obsession

In the 1992 Greek novel (and 2000 worldwide bestseller) Uncle Petros and Goldbach's Conjecture, author Apostolos Doxiadis painted the psychological drama of dedicating one's entire existence to a single unsolved problem.

In 2000, to promote the English release, publishers Faber & Faber and Bloomsbury announced a $1,000,000 cash prize to anyone who could prove Goldbach's conjecture within two years! Thousands of amateur and professional mathematicians submitted attempts, but the prize went unclaimed.

The novel explores a haunting philosophical question: Could Goldbach's conjecture be true, but unprovable?

Challenge 2 How does Kurt Gödel's Incompleteness Theorem relate to Goldbach's Conjecture?
In Peano Arithmetic, if Goldbach were false, there would exist a finite counter-example $N$, which could be verified in finite steps (and thus proven false). Therefore, if Goldbach is undecidable, it must be true!
Challenge 3 Why did the $1,000,000 publisher prize expire in 2002 without a winner?
Because a genuine proof requires revolutionary mathematical machinery beyond contemporary arithmetic techniques—a 2-year deadline was impossible!

📐 Gödelian Undecidability & Arithmetic $\Pi_1$ Sentences

Goldbach's conjecture is a $\Pi_1$ sentence in the arithmetical hierarchy:

\forall n \in \mathbb{N} \; \exists p_1, p_2 \in \mathbb{P} : (2n + 4 = p_1 + p_2)

Because the predicate is bounded-quantifier decidable for each $n$, any counter-example is effectively witnessable. Consequently, if the Peano axioms $\mathsf{PA}$ are consistent and Goldbach is unprovable in $\mathsf{PA}$, then Goldbach must be true in the standard model $\mathbb{N}$!
