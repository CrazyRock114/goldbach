✨ Unpacking the Prime Ratio

A continued fraction expresses a number as a tower of reciprocals. For a fraction $p/q$, the Euclidean algorithm computes:

\frac{p}{q} = a_0 + \frac{1}{a_1 + \frac{1}{a_2 + \frac{1}{\dots + \frac{1}{a_k}}}}

Geometrically, this is identical to taking a rectangle of dimensions $p \times q$ and carving out the largest possible square of size $p \times p$. The remaining sliver is then subdivided, continuing until the rectangle is completely tiled by squares!

The quotients $a_k$ count how many squares of each size fit into the rectangle, illustrating the Euclidean rhythm hidden inside every Goldbach pair.

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Select $11 / 19$. What is its continued fraction expansion?
$19 / 11 = 1 + 8/11$; $11 / 8 = 1 + 3/8$; $8 / 3 = 2 + 2/3$; $3 / 2 = 1 + 1/2$. Thus $11/19 = [0; 1, 1, 2, 1, 2]$!
Challenge 2 Why are the convergents $h_n / k_n$ guaranteed to alternate strictly above and below $p/q$?
By the fundamental continuant identity: $h_n k_{n-1} - h_{n-1} k_n = (-1)^{n-1}$. The sign flips at each step, sandwiching the true ratio between successive lower and upper bounds!
Challenge 3 What happens when $p = q = N/2$?
$p/q = 1$, which has the trivial continued fraction $[1]$. The square dissection is a single square of side $p$!

📐 Best Rational Approximations & Continuants

Let $[a_0; a_1, \dots, a_k]$ be the continued fraction of $p/q$. The convergents satisfy the recurrence:

h_{-1} = 1, h_0 = a_0, \quad h_n = a_n h_{n-1} + h_{n-2}, \qquad k_{-1} = 0, k_0 = 1, \quad k_n = a_n k_{n-1} + k_{n-2}

By Dirichlet's Approximation Theorem, every convergent satisfies $\left| \frac{p}{q} - \frac{h_n}{k_n} \right| < \frac{1}{k_n k_{n+1}} < \frac{1}{k_n^2}$, delivering optimal Diophantine approximations to prime ratios.
