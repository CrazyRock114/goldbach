✨ Resource Partitioning Under Scarcity

In survival logistics, discrete packages cannot be subdivided arbitrarily. If rations are packed in fixed prime-size bundles $\{3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23\}$, reaching an exact even quota is identical to discovering a Goldbach pair.

Notice what happens if you select a pair whose sum is even 1 calorie above or below the target: metabolic shock ensues! Exactness is mandatory.

Because every even number up to 50 has multiple valid pairs (e.g. $28 = 5+23 = 11+17$), smart survivors conserve rare large primes for later high-energy days!

📐 The Knapsack Problem with Prime Weights

The standard 0-1 Knapsack Problem is NP-complete. Restricting capacity to an even integer $N$ and item values to the prime set $\mathbb{P}$ with cardinality bound $k=2$ yields the exact Goldbach query:

\max \sum_{i=1}^m p_i x_i \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \sum_{i=1}^m x_i = 2, \quad x_i \in \{0, 1, 2\}, \quad \sum p_i x_i = N

For any even integer $N \ge 4$, the feasible region is guaranteed non-empty under Goldbach's Conjecture, admitting linear-time resolution via binary search over prime tables.
