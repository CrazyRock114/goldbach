✨ Intuitive Concept: The 3-Piece Jigsaw

In Lab 009, we covered even strips with 2 prime dominoes. Now, our strips have odd lengths ($11, 15, 21, 27$).

Because $\text{Odd} + \text{Odd} = \text{Even}$, you can NEVER cover an odd strip with two odd primes! You need THREE dominoes ($p_1 + p_2 + p_3 = \text{Odd}$).

Helfgott's theorem guarantees that no matter what odd level you pick, there is ALWAYS a winning 3-domino solution!

Challenge 2 On Target 15, find a solution using three identical prime dominoes.
Three 5-dominoes: 5 + 5 + 5 = 15!
Challenge 3 Why does adding a 3rd domino make the puzzle so much easier than with 2 dominoes?
Because with 2 dominoes, picking the first domino fixes the second domino completely. With 3 dominoes, you have two free choices before the third is determined, giving vastly more combinations!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

A ternary prime tiling is a triple $(p_1, p_2, p_3) \in \mathbb{P}^3$ such that:

[0, p_1] \cup [p_1, p_1+p_2] \cup [p_1+p_2, p_1+p_2+p_3] = [0, M]

with pairwise disjoint interior intervals, establishing the linear Diophantine relation $p_1 + p_2 + p_3 = M$.
