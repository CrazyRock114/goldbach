✨ Intuitive Concept: The Smallest Key

If you have a keyring of prime keys ($2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19...$), which key is the first one to unlock the lock for $N$?

For almost every even number, the very first key you try—3—works immediately! (e.g. $6=3+3, 8=3+5, 10=3+7, 14=3+11, 16=3+13$).

The first number where 3 fails is 12 (since $12-3=9$, composite), requiring $p=5$ ($5+7$). And the famous record holder under 100 is 98, which resists 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, and 17, finally surrendering to 19 ($19 + 79 = 98$)!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Why does $p = 3$ fail for $N = 98$?
$98 - 3 = 95 = 5 \times 19$ (composite!).
Challenge 2 Why does $p = 5$ fail for $N = 98$?
$98 - 5 = 93 = 3 \times 31$ (composite!).
Challenge 3 Why does $p = 7$ fail for $N = 98$?
$98 - 7 = 91 = 7 \times 13$ (composite!). In fact, $98 - 11 = 87 = 3 \times 29$, $98 - 13 = 85 = 5 \times 17$, $98 - 17 = 81 = 3^4$. All fail until $98 - 19 = 79$ (PRIME)!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

Define the least Goldbach prime functional:

p_{\min}(N) = \min \{p \in \mathbb{P} \mid N - p \in \mathbb{P}\}

Under the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH), it is conjectured that $p_{\min}(N) = O(\ln^2 N \ln \ln N)$. Unconditionally, Baker, Harman, and Pintz sieve methods bound $p_{\min}(N) \ll N^{0.525}$.
