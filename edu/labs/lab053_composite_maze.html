✨ Prime Stepping Stones

In this maze, the number line is folded into a 2D floorplan. The dungeon walls are made of composite numbers (like 4, 9, 15, 21, 25), while the safe walkways are forged from primes.

As you step from prime to prime, notice how twin primes (3 and 5, 5 and 7, 11 and 13) form adjacent stepping corridors, whereas long runs of composites create impenetrable obstacles!

Every step updates your Prime Key, testing your ability to dynamically identify prime neighbors and avoid tempting composite dead-ends.

📐 Prime Gaps & Discrete Connectivity

Let $C_n = [n, n+k]$ be a sequence of consecutive composite numbers. Such sequences can be arbitrarily long (for example, $(k+1)! + 2, (k+1)! + 3, \dots, (k+1)! + k + 1$ are all composite).

\limsup_{n \to \infty} \frac{p_{n+1} - p_n}{\ln p_n} \ge 1

In any discrete spatial embedding of integers, composite clustering creates geometric percolation thresholds. Path existence across prime networks directly mirrors the bounded gaps theorems established by Yitang Zhang (2013) and James Maynard.
