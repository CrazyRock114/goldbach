✨ Lev Schnirelmann's 1930 Breakthrough

For nearly two centuries after Goldbach's 1742 letter, mathematicians could not even prove that every number was the sum of one million primes!

In 1930, 25-year-old Soviet mathematician Lev Schnirelmann invented a revolutionary concept: Schnirelmann Density. He proved that if a set of integers has positive density, its repeated sumset $A + A + \dots + A$ eventually captures 100% of all integers!

Using Brun's sieve, Schnirelmann proved that the primes form an additive basis of finite order $C \le 300,000$. This broke the psychological barrier, proving that primes are rich enough to generate all numbers additively!

Weak Goldbach proves every odd number $\ge 7$ is the sum of 3 primes! Since 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 are covered by 1 or 2 primes, every single integer $\ge 2$ is covered by at most 3 primes!

📐 Formal Definition of Additive Basis & Sumsets

Let $A \subseteq \mathbb{N}_0$. The $h$-fold sumset is $hA = \{a_1 + \dots + a_h \mid a_i \in A\}$. $A$ is an additive basis of order $h$ if $hA = \mathbb{N}_0$.

Schnirelmann defined density $\sigma(A) = \inf_{n \ge 1} \frac{A(n)}{n}$. His Fundamental Theorem states:

\sigma(A + B) \ge \sigma(A) + \sigma(B) - \sigma(A)\sigma(B) \implies \sigma(A) > 0 \implies \exists h \in \mathbb{N}: hA = \mathbb{N}

By Helfgott (2013) and Terence Tao (2012), the primes are an additive basis of order at most 3 for all integers $n \ge 2$.
