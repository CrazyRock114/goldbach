✨ Intuitive Concept: Territories of Prime Gravity

A Voronoi diagram divides a map into territories like countries around capital cities: every point on the map belongs to whichever seed is closest.

In this lab, our seed capitals are the Goldbach prime coordinates $(p, N - p)$. The colorful cells show the sphere of influence of each prime decomposition.

Notice how every single point on the board is claimed by a prime pair territory: there are zero unclaimed islands or deserts!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Move $N$ to 30. How many Voronoi territories are carved out?
Since $N = 30$ has 3 unordered pairs ($(7, 23)$, $(11, 19)$, $(13, 17)$), there are 6 ordered seed points in the plane ($(7, 23), (23, 7)$, etc.), carving the space into 6 symmetrical Voronoi cells!
Challenge 2 What happens when $N$ only has 1 pair (like $N = 12$)?
The plane is divided by a single straight perpendicular line between $(5, 7)$ and $(7, 5)$!
Challenge 3 Why are the Voronoi cells completely symmetric across the line $y = x$?
Because for every seed $(p, q)$, there is an identical reflection seed $(q, p)$! The seed set is invariant under reflection across $y = x$.

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

Let $S_N = \{(p, N-p) \mid p \in \mathbb{P}, N-p \in \mathbb{P}\} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ be the finite set of sites. The Voronoi cell for site $s_i \in S_N$ is:

V(s_i) = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid \|x - s_i\| \le \|x - s_j\| \quad \forall j \neq i\}

The cells form a convex polygonal tessellation of $\mathbb{R}^2$ with reflection symmetry group $\mathbb{Z}_2 = \{I, \tau\}$.
