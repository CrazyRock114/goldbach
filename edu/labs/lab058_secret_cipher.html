How does the Goldbach Cipher work?

  • Each letter of the alphabet has an index: $\text{A}=1, \text{B}=2, \dots, \text{Z}=26$.
  • To encrypt letter $L$, the cipher selects an even target $N$ and chooses a prime $p$ such that the difference $(N - 2p) \equiv L \pmod{26}$.
  • The encrypted token is the pair $[N : p]$.

Notice the built-in authenticity check: An eavesdropper who tries to tamper with the tokens will accidentally produce numbers where $q = N - p$ is composite! Decryption instantly rejects corrupted tokens as mathematical forgeries.

📐 Homophonic Prime Mapping

Let $\Sigma = \{A, \dots, Z\}$ be the alphabet and $\tau : \Sigma \to \mathbb{Z}_{26}$ the standard injection. The encryption relation $E(m)$ maps character $m$ to an element of the preimage set:

E(m) = \left\{ (N, p) \in 2\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{P} \mid N - p \in \mathbb{P} \text{ and } (N - 2p) \equiv \tau(m) \pmod{26} \right\}

Because the partition count $g(N) \to \infty$ as $N \to \infty$, the cardinality $|E(m)|$ is unbounded, providing dynamic nonces that prevent codebook dictionary attacks.
