The Final Grand Goldbach Quest

Mastery Question 1: Why does $2N = 90$ have significantly more Goldbach pairs than $2N = 64$?

$90$ has prime factors $2, 3, 5$ ($90 = 2 \times 3^2 \times 5$). Every odd prime factor $p$ boosts the singular series product factor $\frac{p-1}{p-2}$, whereas $64 = 2^6$ has NO odd prime factors, giving it the lowest possible Goldbach richness!

Mastery Question 2: What connects the Weak Goldbach Conjecture (Helfgott 2013) to the Strong Binary Conjecture?

If the Strong Binary Conjecture is true, Weak Goldbach follows immediately: for any odd $M \ge 7$, subtract $3$ to get an even number $M - 3 = p_1 + p_2$, so $M = p_1 + p_2 + 3$ is a sum of 3 primes!

The Asymptotic Guarantee: Hardy-Littlewood

The Hardy-Littlewood asymptotic formula for the number of representations:

$$g(2N) \sim 2 C_2 \prod_{p \mid N, p > 2} \left(\frac{p-1}{p-2}\right) \int_2^{2N} \frac{dx}{\ln(x) \ln(2N-x)}$$

Because the twin prime constant $2 C_2 \approx 1.3203 > 0$ and the integral diverges as $\Theta\left(\frac{2N}{\ln^2(2N)}\right)$, the conjecture is asymptotically guaranteed to have infinite partitions for all sufficiently large $2N$.
