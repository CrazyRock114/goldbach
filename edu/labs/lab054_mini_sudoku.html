Lab 054: Goldbach Mini-Sudoku

Fill the 4×4 grid exclusively with Prime Numbers {2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13} so that every 2×1 block or row pair sums to the designated Goldbach even target!

Prime Keypad

✨ Prime Grid Constraints

In standard Sudoku, digits 1 through 9 are constrained purely by permutation. In Goldbach Sudoku, numbers must be strictly prime and meet additive constraints.

For example, if a 2-cell block must sum to 16, and you know both cells are prime, the only possible unordered pairs from our prime bank are $(3, 13)$ and $(5, 11)$. A single given digit immediately forces its partner by the involution $q = N - p$!

Deductive reasoning in this puzzle exercises both linear Diophantine constraints and prime pattern recognition.

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 If a 2-cell row target is 12 and one cell contains 5, what must the other cell contain?
$12 - 5 = \mathbf{7}$. Since 7 is prime, $(5, 7)$ is the only solution containing 5!
Challenge 2 Can 2 be placed in any block whose target sum is 14?
No! Because $14 - 2 = 12$, which is composite ($2^2 \times 3$). Only odd primes can sum to even numbers $\ge 6$!
Challenge 3 Solve Puzzle 3 with zero error checks.
Start with the cells that intersect both a row target of 24 and a column target of 20. The intersection pinpoints the single prime shared by both decompositions.

📐 Additive Graph Embedding & Coloring

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a bipartite constraint graph where vertices $v_i \in V$ represent grid cells and edges $e = (v_i, v_j) \in E$ enforce the equation:

x_i + x_j = N_e, \quad x_i, x_j \in \mathbb{P}

Since the Goldbach set of each even target $|S(N_e)|$ is finite and small for small $N_e$, the constraint satisfaction problem (CSP) possesses unique deterministic propagation cascades akin to exact cover algorithms.
