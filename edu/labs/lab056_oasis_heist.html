✨ The Science of Primorial Oases

Why did the Phantom prefer 150 over 128 or 142? Because 150 is divisible by both 3 and 5 ($150 = 30 \times 5$).

When an even number is divisible by an odd prime $p$, no prime partner candidate can be eliminated by the congruence $q \equiv 0 \pmod p$. Instead, both residue classes modulo $p$ remain available!

For $p=3$, this gives a bonus factor of $\frac{3-1}{3-2} = \mathbf{2.0\times}$. For $p=5$, an extra $\frac{5-1}{5-2} = \mathbf{1.333\times}$. Multiplied together, multiples of 30 receive a theoretical boost of $2.0 \times 1.333 = \mathbf{2.67\times}$ more pairs than powers of 2!

📐 The Hardy-Littlewood Singular Series Formula

The number of Goldbach representations $g(N)$ is governed asymptotically by the singular series $\mathfrak{S}(N)$:

\mathfrak{S}(N) = 2 C_2 \prod_{p \mid N, p > 2} \frac{p-1}{p-2}, \quad C_2 = \prod_{p > 2} \left(1 - \frac{1}{(p-1)^2}\right) \approx 0.66016

For any even number coprime to 3 and 5 (such as $2^k$), $\mathfrak{S}(N) = 2 C_2 \approx 1.32$. But when $30 \mid N$, $\mathfrak{S}(N) = 2 C_2 \times 2 \times \frac{4}{3} \approx 3.52$. The ratio is exactly $\frac{8}{3} = 2.667$, creating the upper envelope peaks of the Goldbach comet.
