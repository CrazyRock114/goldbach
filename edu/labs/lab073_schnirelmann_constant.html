Lab 073: Schnirelmann's Constant: The 300,000 to 3 Plummet

How many primes does it take to build ANY integer? In 1930, Lev Schnirelmann proved at most 300,000. Over 83 years, mathematicians drove this cosmic ceiling down to 3! Step through the timeline of discoveries.

Year: 1930
🏛️
Milestone 1 of 6

Lev Schnirelmann

Schnirelmann Constant C ≤ 300,000

Invented Schnirelmann density and proved that primes form an additive basis of finite order. For the first time in human history, every integer was proven to be the sum of at most a finite number of primes!

Logarithmic Plummet of Schnirelmann's Bound C (1930–2013):

✨ From 300,000 to the Final Frontier

Why was Schnirelmann's initial bound of 300,000 celebrated as a world-shaking triumph even though 300,000 is enormous?

Because before 1930, humanity had no proof that the number of primes was bounded at all! It was conceivable that as numbers grew to $10^{100}$, they might require millions or billions of primes to assemble.

Once a finite ceiling was established, mathematicians attacked the constant using increasingly sophisticated sieves and the circle method. Over 83 years, the bound collapsed from $300,000 \to 26 \to 7 \to 6 \to \mathbf{3}$!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Why was Olivier Ramaré's 1995 result ($C \le 7$) considered a landmark?
Ramaré proved that every even number is the sum of at most 6 primes! Adding one more prime (or 1) immediately proved every integer is the sum of at most 7 primes for all numbers down to 2.
Challenge 2 What was Terence Tao's contribution in 2012?
Terence Tao proved that every odd integer $>1$ is the sum of at most 5 primes, setting the stage for Helfgott's final closure just one year later!
Challenge 3 If Strong Goldbach is eventually proven, what will Schnirelmann's constant become for all numbers $\ge 2$?
Since every even is $\le 2$ primes and every odd is $\le 3$ primes (odd $- 3 =$ even), the ultimate universal constant for all integers $\ge 2$ will be permanently fixed at 3.

📐 Mathematical Convergence of Additive Order

Let $C_{\text{Sch}} = \min \{h \in \mathbb{N} \mid h(\mathbb{P} \cup \{0, 1\}) = \mathbb{N}_0\}$.

300000 \xrightarrow[\text{1930}]{\text{Schnirelmann}} 6\times 10^{10} \xrightarrow[\text{1976}]{\text{Vaughan}} 26 \xrightarrow[\text{1995}]{\text{Ramaré}} 7 \xrightarrow[\text{2012}]{\text{Tao}} 6 \xrightarrow[\text{2013}]{\text{Helfgott}} 3

Helfgott's theorem establishes that $3(\mathbb{P} \cup \{0, 1\}) = \mathbb{N}_0$ holds unconditionally, completely closing the weak Schnirelmann basis problem.
