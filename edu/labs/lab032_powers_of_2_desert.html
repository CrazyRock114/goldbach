Lab 032: The Desert of Powers of 2 ($2^k$)

Powers of 2 ($16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512...$) are the arid deserts of number theory. Why do they form the strict bottom floor of the Goldbach comet?

Select Power of 2:
Bonus Factor: 1.00× (Zero Bonus)
Current Power of 2:
N = 16 (2⁴)
Actual Pairs: 2 pairs (3+13, 5+11)

✨ Intuitive Concept: The Bare Floor

Every number that contains an odd prime factor (like 3, 5, or 7) gets an extra boost of Goldbach pairs because candidate primes avoid that prime divisor.

Powers of 2 ($2^k$) have NO odd prime factors whatsoever ($16 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2$). They get zero boost multipliers! Because of this, powers of 2 sit at the absolute minimum bottom baseline of the Goldbach comet.

Yet even in this desolate desert with zero multipliers, the number of pairs steadily grows! As $k$ increases, $g(2^k)$ climbs: $g(16)=2$, $g(32)=2$, $g(64)=5$, $g(128)=3$, $g(256)=9$, $g(512)=11$, $g(1024)=24$!

🎯 Try This! (Hands-On Challenges)

Challenge 1 Compare $N = 64$ ($2^6$, 5 pairs) with its neighbor $N = 66$ ($2 \times 3 \times 11$).
$N = 66$ has 6 pairs (more than 64) because 66 is divisible by 3 and 11, giving it a singular series multiplier of $2 \times \frac{10}{9} = 2.22\times$!
Challenge 2 Inspect $N = 1024$ ($2^{10}$). How many pairs survive in the desert?
Even without any prime bonuses, $N = 1024$ has 24 distinct Goldbach pairs! This proves that raw size alone guarantees abundance as $N \to \infty$.
Challenge 3 Why can a power of 2 never be divisible by 3?
Because $2 \equiv -1 \pmod 3$, so $2^k \equiv (-1)^k \equiv 1$ or $2 \pmod 3$. The remainder is never 0, guaranteeing that powers of 2 never get the 2.0× multiple-of-6 bonus!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

For $N = 2^k$ ($k \ge 2$), the prime factor set is $\{2\}$. The singular series product reduces to the empty product:

\prod_{p \mid 2^k, p > 2} \frac{p-1}{p-2} = 1 \implies \mathfrak{S}(2^k) = 2 C_2 \approx 1.3203

This is the absolute infimum of $\mathfrak{S}(N)$ over all even integers $N \ge 4$, formally establishing that powers of 2 define the asymptotic lower envelope $g(N) \ge C_2 \frac{N}{\ln^2 N}$.
