✨ Intuitive Concept: The Mountain Peaks

A primorial is formed by multiplying consecutive primes: $2$, $2 \times 3 = 6$, $2 \times 3 \times 5 = 30$, $2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7 = 210$.

Every new prime factor multiplies the number of open residue channels by $\frac{p-1}{p-2}$. For example:

  • Factor 3 multiplies by $2.00\times$.
  • Factor 5 multiplies by $1.33\times$ (Total: $2.67\times$).
  • Factor 7 multiplies by $1.20\times$ (Total: $3.20\times$).
  • Factor 11 multiplies by $1.11\times$ (Total: $3.56\times$).

Because they collect all possible prime multipliers, primorials form the highest soaring peaks of the Goldbach comet, leaving surrounding numbers far below!

Challenge 1 Select $P_4\# = 210$. How many Goldbach pairs does 210 have compared to $208$ and $212$?
$N = 210$ has 19 distinct Goldbach pairs! In contrast, $N = 208$ has only 7 pairs, and $N = 212$ has only 6 pairs. 210 has nearly 3× more pairs than its neighbors!
Challenge 2 Does the primorial boost factor ever reach a finite maximum ceiling?
No! By Mertens' third theorem, the product $\prod_{p \le z} \frac{p-1}{p-2}$ diverges to infinity like $O(\ln \ln N)$. The upper envelope grows unboundedly!
Challenge 3 Select $N = 420 = 2 \times 210$. Why does 420 have so many pairs?
Because $420$ inherits all the prime divisors of 210 (2, 3, 5, 7) while having twice the total length, giving it an astounding 31 distinct Goldbach pairs!

📐 Mathematical Rigor & Formal Definition

For the primorial $P_k\# = \prod_{i=1}^k p_i$, the singular series evaluates to:

\mathfrak{S}(P_k\#) = 2 C_2 \prod_{i=2}^k \frac{p_i - 1}{p_i - 2}

By Mertens' theorem, $\mathfrak{S}(P_k\#) \sim 2 C_2 e^\gamma \ln(p_k) \asymp \ln \ln(P_k\#)$. Thus, the upper envelope of $g(N)$ satisfies $g(N) = O\left(\frac{N \ln \ln N}{\ln^2 N}\right)$.
